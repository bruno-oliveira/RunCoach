# RESEARCH DIGEST


## RUNNA — Runna training methodology, plan structure & adaptation

Runna is a premium running-coaching app (~2M monthly users, acquired by Strava in April 2025, ~$120/yr or £15.99/mo) that generates personalized, structured race-training plans built by human coaches (some former Olympians), with AI used mainly to monitor progress and surface adaptation suggestions rather than to write workouts.

METHODOLOGY & PERIODIZATION: Plans use a two-phase periodization model: (1) a Base Training phase to build aerobic foundation and weekly mileage gradually, with a recommended minimum of 2 (ideally 3) strength sessions/week; then (2) a Key Training Block where interval/tempo volume and intensity increase week-on-week. Key block length is distance-scaled: 5-10K = 8-10 weeks, Half = 10-12 weeks, Marathon = 14-16 weeks, Ultra = 16-20 weeks. With extra runway, base weeks are prepended (e.g., a 24-week marathon timeline = 8 base + 16 key). The whole plan ends with a taper before race day. Philosophy emphasizes "consistency is key" and steady improvement over rigid goal-time targeting.

PACE PERSONALIZATION: A single "estimated current race time" is the master input. The runner provides one benchmark (a 5K/10K/Half time, or it is derived from synced run data and experience level), and Runna projects an equivalent fitness level across ALL distances to set every workout's pace target or range. Workouts carry specific pace targets/ranges (with effort/RPE fallback when needed). Runners can hand-tune via Plan > Manage Plan > Current Race Times in small 15-30s increments. Paces auto-update when a new PB is achieved.

WORKOUT TYPES: Easy run, Long run, Recovery run, Intervals, Tempo, Hills, Strides (10-20s bursts), Time Trial, plus Walk-Run for beginners. Sessions are fully structured (warm-up ~10-15 min easy + drills/strides, work intervals with recovery jogs, cool-down ~10-15 min) and push to watch step-by-step (Garmin, COROS, Apple Watch, Suunto, Fitbit) and Strava.

CONFIGURATION: Three independent dials. (a) Training Focus: Endurance / Speed / Balanced — shapes session mix. (b) Training Volume: Gradual / Steady / Progressive — controls weekly-mileage ramp; advanced mode sets separate max distances for easy runs, hard sessions, and long runs. (c) Training Difficulty: Comfortable / Balanced / Challenging — controls number and intensity of hard sessions (hills/intervals/tempo) and long-run challenge. Runners pick 2-6 run days/week and freely place long runs (not locked to weekends).

ADAPTATION: Three mechanisms. (1) Mileage Insights (race plans only): tracks all runs incl. Free Runs/Instant Workouts, compares completed vs planned mileage/training load, and recommends changes to weekly mileage, long-run mileage, or runs/week — ramping back gradually after misses or raising volume when consistently exceeding targets. (2) Plan Realignment: triggers (usually Mondays) when >3 workouts or a full week is missed, offering Skip / Rearrange / Extend plan (if no fixed date) / Rebuild to same end date / Continue; 4+ weeks missed offers full restart/new plan. (3) Post-workout thumbs up/down feedback ("paces too tough," "too long," "not 100%") — currently used mostly for product-side plan tuning, with per-runner dynamic adaptation positioned as future. Pace targets also auto-adjust on PBs.

STRENGTH: Personalized strength, conditioning, mobility and Pilates programs included in subscription, customizable by frequency, session length (e.g., 25-min options), and equipment (bodyweight/dumbbells), with demo videos and run-specific focus.

PRAISE vs CRITICISM: Praised for structure, watch sync (zero-thinking execution), flexibility (movable days, RPE swaps), value, breadth (Couch-to-5K free plan through ultra), and documented PBs/first finishes; Apple App of the Year finalist 2024. Criticized for plans/progressions being too aggressive for some — physical therapists report regular Runna-linked overuse injuries (stress fractures, shin splints, Achilles tendinopathy); the algorithm "takes the runner at their word" and can over-ramp novices, recovery between hard sessions is sometimes tight, it lacks population-level overtraining detection, limited training-philosophy diversity, and intensity-dialing-down features were added reactively (no rigorous study proves elevated injury rates).

## RUNMOTION — RunMotion Coach: methodology, plan structure, and adaptation

RunMotion Coach is a French-built adaptive running app whose algorithm was designed by Guillaume Adam (former French national-team athlete, certified coach, MIT/CNRS researcher, co-author of an MIT running-performance-prediction paper) and refined yearly since 2018 on data from thousands of runners. Its differentiator is a physiology-grounded performance model rather than fixed 8/12-week templates. Training paces are derived from two core parameters: MAS/vVO2max (Maximal Aerobic Speed, the velocity at VO2max, sustainable 4-7 min, estimated via a half-Cooper 6-minute test = distance_m/100, or back-calculated from two recent race results) and an endurance index. RunMotion uses two endurance formulations: (1) the Peronnet-Thibault slope EI = (%vVO2max held in race - 100) / ln(race_time_min / 6), where values near 0 (e.g. -5) mean strong endurance and -10 means poor; and (2) a refined MIT/CNRS 1-10 "long-distance endurance" scale = (time sustainable at 90% vVO2max) / (time at vVO2max, fixed at 6 min), where 10 = ~60 min at 90% MAS, 5 = ~30 min, 3 = low. Crucially, paces are computed per session theme using predicted race time for that distance (a 6x1000m "at 10K pace" session pulls the runner's theoretical 10K time directly) rather than a one-size-fits-all %MAS table, which lets two runners with identical MAS but different endurance get different marathon/half paces. Plans are reverse-periodized from race day: a 16-20 week start runs through earlier (more speed-focused) phases before entering a final ~12-week race-specific block, with the last ~2 weeks always a lighter taper; shorter 7-9 week starts auto-compress. Adaptation works on trends, not single sessions: after each workout the runner rates perceived effort/how they felt, and the app recalculates the upcoming week, reducing load when fatigued, accounting for whether you're in a heavy vs. down week and whether the acute-to-chronic workload ratio is stable, and reshaping the schedule around missed sessions and availability. It covers 5K/10K/half/marathon road, trail/ultra (with weekly target elevation gain over the 4 months pre-race and "shock weekend" planning), triathlon (XS-XXL), plus beginner walk-run (6 weeks) and wellness/weight-loss goals. Premium adds strength & conditioning (50+ exercises), stretching, mental prep, nutrition, HR zones, and cycling/swimming.

**Methodology:** Performed iterative web search and direct page fetches against RunMotion's official English site (run-motion.com), its help center (support.run-motion.com), and app store listings. Prioritized RunMotion's own methodology/science pages (MAS, endurance index, intensity zones, pace calculation, ChatGPT-vs-coach comparison) for concrete formulas and percentages, cross-referenced the founder/science claims, and pulled strengths/weaknesses from app-store and review summaries. Some specifics (exact subscription tiers, full per-zone pace tables) are approximate where RunMotion publishes ranges rather than exact figures, and deeper Reddit/independent reviews were sparse in results.

**Adaptation:** RunMotion adapts on trends rather than single events. After each session the runner submits a perceived-effort / how-it-felt rating; the app then recalculates the upcoming week's load and paces using that feedback plus synced watch data (second-by-second from GPS watch, prioritized over Strava's lap-by-lap). Load management is explicit: it tracks weekly load and monitors the acute-to-chronic workload ratio, reduces load when fatigue is detected, and contextualizes a missed workout by whether the runner is in a heavy or down week before reacting (a single skipped session does not trigger overcorrection). Training paces are re-derived from updated MAS + endurance index as fitness changes, and the schedule reshapes around the runner's chosen training days, long-run/interval-day preferences, and availability. Periodization is elastic and reverse-built from race day (final ~12-week race-specific block with a ~2-week taper), auto-compressing for short lead times. This maps closely to RunCoach's own adaptation engine (effort trends, adherence, future-week adjustment) but RunMotion adds an explicit ACWR signal, endurance-index-personalized paces, and per-session-distance pace lookups that RunCoach can borrow.

**Key features:**
- Physiology-grounded two-parameter model: MAS/vVO2max + endurance index, designed by an MIT/CNRS researcher-coach (Guillaume Adam), refined yearly since 2018 on thousands of runners
- MAS estimated from a 6-min half-Cooper test (distance_m / 100) or back-solved from two recent race results; values ~10 km/h beginner to >24 km/h elite
- Endurance index: Peronnet-Thibault slope EI = (%vVO2max held - 100)/ln(race_min/6), plus a 1-10 long-distance scale = time at 90% vVO2max / 6 min (10 = ~60 min at 90% MAS)
- Per-session-theme pace derivation: a workout 'at 10K pace' pulls the runner's predicted 10K time directly, not a generic %MAS lookup, so endurance differences change race-specific paces
- Five intensity zones by %MAS: Z1 high fundamental endurance 0-60%, Z2 active/aerobic-threshold 60-70%, Z3 70-79% (~marathon), Z4 anaerobic threshold 79-88% (~half), Z5 VO2max 88-100% (5K/10K); easy/fundamental endurance set ~60-65% MAS
- Reverse periodization from race date: 16-20 wk start uses earlier speed-focused phases into a final ~12-week race-specific block; last ~2 weeks always a lighter taper; 7-9 wk starts auto-compress instead of fixed templates
- Trend-based adaptation: post-session perceived-effort feedback recalculates the next week; reduces load when fatigued; considers heavy vs down week and acute-to-chronic workload ratio stability; tolerates single missed sessions
- Race-distance coverage: 5K/10K/half/marathon, trail/ultra with weekly target elevation gain (4 mo pre-race) and 'shock weekend', triathlon XS-XXL, beginner walk-run (6 wk), wellness/weight-loss
- Configurable schedule: choose training days, when to place long run and interval session, plus 1-2 weekly cross-training (cycling) and 1-2 strength sessions; weekly reminder to update training days
- Data sync: GPS watch gives second-by-second data (prioritized), Strava only lap-by-lap; Strava edits sync back but Garmin/Suunto/Polar edits do not; supports menstrual-cycle integration
- Premium (~$80-150/yr) adds strength & conditioning (50+ exercises), stretching, mental prep, nutrition, HR zones, swim/bike; selectable coach personality (motivating/caring/serious); 7-day free trial

- Reverse/elastic periodization that auto-compresses or expands phases based on weeks-to-race (e.g. 7-9 wk vs 16-20 wk) into a final ~12-week race-specific block, instead of distance-keyed minimum-week templates
- Trail/ultra support with weekly target vertical-gain prescription over the 4 months pre-race and 'shock weekend' (back-to-back big days) planning; triathlon multi-sport planning
- Cross-modal training integration: built-in strength & conditioning (50+ exercises), stretching, mental preparation, and cycling/swimming sessions woven into the week
- Multi-goal season planning (3-4 A-races/year spaced >=2 months with intermediate races as training blocks) rather than a single-race plan
- Menstrual-cycle-aware planning and selectable coach personality / tone of coaching

## SCIENCE — Modern endurance training science and how leading adaptive apps operationalize it into auto-adjusting running plans

Consensus across coaching literature and sports-science reviews converges on a small set of encodable rules. (1) DANIELS VDOT: a single fitness number derived from a recent race that sets five pace zones tied to physiology — Easy E (~59-74% VO2max / ~65-79% HRmax, the bulk of mileage), Marathon M (~75-84% VO2max), Threshold T (~83-88% VO2max / ~88-92% HRmax, "comfortably hard," ~1hr race effort, run as 20-min tempos or 5-15 min cruise intervals with short rest), Interval I (~95-100% VO2max, 3-5 min reps with ~equal recovery to maximize time at VO2max), and Repetition R (~105-110%, fast 200-400m / <2 min reps with FULL recovery for economy/speed). Daniels caps quality: T <= ~10% of weekly miles, I <= ~8%, and overall ~70-80% E, ~10-15% M+T, ~10-15% I+R. (2) INTENSITY DISTRIBUTION: most well-trained/elite athletes train ~80% easy (below LT1) year-round; the debate is the other 20%. Pyramidal (~75-80% Z1 / 15-20% Z2 / 2-10% Z3) dominates base/early season and is the natural elite default; Polarized (~75-80% Z1 / ~5% Z2 / 15-20% Z3, avoiding the LT1-LT2 "grey zone") shows the largest gains in controlled trials of already-trained athletes and suits the sharpening/competition phase. The unifying rule is "80/20": keep ~80% of SESSIONS or volume easy regardless of model. (3) PERIODIZATION & TAPER: block periodization with progressive overload; the best-supported taper is a ~14-day, exponential/step reduction of VOLUME by 41-60% while KEEPING intensity high and frequency >= ~80%, yielding ~2-3% (up to ~6%) performance gain. (4) PROGRESSION & INJURY: the old "10%/week" rule is a soft heuristic (good default ceiling, not literal law); it's been supplanted/supplemented by the Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR = ~7-day load / ~28-day load), with a commonly cited "sweet spot" of 0.8-1.3 and elevated risk above ~1.5 — though recent literature seriously disputes the sweet spot's validity for individual sports, so it should be a soft flag, not a hard gate. (5) LONG RUN: ~20-30% of weekly volume (Daniels caps at 25-30% AND a 2.5-3 hr time limit); progress long run ~10-15%/week. (6) SUPERCOMPENSATION/DELOAD: insert a recovery/down week every 3-4 weeks, cutting volume ~20-40% (keep some intensity), to let fatigue dissipate and bank adaptation; supercompensation peaks ~1-2 weeks post-deload. (7) PMC / BANISTER: model fitness/fatigue/form as exponentially-weighted moving averages of daily training stress (TSS/RSS) — CTL ("Fitness") with a 42-day time constant, ATL ("Fatigue") with a 7-day time constant, and TSB ("Form") = CTL - ATL. TSB > +10 = fresh/race-ready, -10 to +10 = neutral, < -10 = fatigued; sustainable CTL ramp is ~5-7 TSS/day/week. Leading apps operationalize these: TrainingPeaks exposes the raw PMC (CTL/ATL/TSB) and recommends racing at positive TSB; Garmin (Firstbeat) reframes the same math as Acute Load (~7d), Chronic Load (~4wk), a personalized Load Ratio "optimal range," Training Status (Productive/Maintaining/Overreaching/Detraining via load+VO2max trend) and Training Readiness (sleep, HRV, recovery, stress) to auto-adjust Daily Suggested Workouts and adaptive plans day-by-day; Stryd uses running POWER with auto-calculated Critical Power (CP, the ~30-70 min sustainable power that anchors zones) and Running Stress Score (RSS, like TSS but power/biomechanically weighted), ramping load ~5-10%/week, peaking ~2/3 through the block, then a sharp taper; Runna layers an algorithm over coach-designed templates that adjusts paces via "Pace Insights" (monitors consistency hitting interval/tempo paces and shifts them to sustainable-but-challenging), grows the long run ~10-15%/week, balances whole-week mileage and intensity, offers Progressive/Steady/Gradual volume tracks, and reflows around missed/moved sessions.</summary>
