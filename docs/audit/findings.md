# RUNCOACH AUDIT — FULL FINDINGS DIGEST


## PERIODIZATION — Periodization & mileage progression

**How it works:** Phase distribution is computed in `app/core/training/phase_calculator.py::calculate_phases`. A distance-specific profile (`_ROAD_PHASE_PROFILES`, e.g. Marathon base 0.30 / build 0.35 / peak 0.15 with a fixed 3-week taper; 5K with a 2-week taper) sets percentage splits; taper is clamped to `min(profile_taper, max(1, weeks//4))`, the remaining weeks are split base/build/peak with floors (base>=2, build>=2, peak>=1), then trimmed/padded to match `weeks`. `is_recovery_week` decides deload weeks per-phase: every 4th week WITHIN base/build (only if that phase is >=4 weeks per the gate), the 3rd week of a 4+ week peak, never in taper. Mileage is built in `mileage_progression.py::calculate_weekly_progression`: `get_peak_mileage` derives a target peak from `current_km * per-distance multiplier`, bounded by a `1 + 1.5*(weeks/16)` (capped 2.6) factor, VDOT and ACWR adjustments, and an absolute `MAX_PEAK_MILEAGE` ceiling. Base ramps `current_km`→0.70*peak, build ramps 0.70*peak→peak, peak oscillates 0.97-0.99*peak, all clamped by a per-step week-over-week cap (`WEEK_OVER_WEEK_CAP`=1.10, adjusted by volume trend). Recovery weeks dip to `RECOVERY_WEEK_RATIO`=0.65 of a separately-tracked high-water mark so the post-deload ramp resumes from the pre-deload level. Taper scales straight from peak via `_get_taper_curve` (e.g. marathon [0.85,0.70,0.50]). A second, independent high-water 10% cap in `plan_generator.py:141-181` re-clamps the assembled week by scaling only flexible (easy/long) workouts. Tuning constants live in `tuning.py`; trail/ultra plans dispatch through bracket/elevation profiles.

**Strengths:** High-water-mark tracking (mileage_progression.py:203-246, 318-435) is genuinely well done: recovery weeks dip to 65% but do not reset the ramp baseline, so progression resumes from the pre-deload load rather than re-climbing from the dip — this is the correct way to model deload/supercompensation and avoids the classic 'sawtooth that never progresses' bug. | The 10%/week rule is enforced in two layers: per-step inside the progression (_ramp_week_km, _progress_peak_phase) and again at the assembled-week level against actual_high_water in plan_generator.py:141, which correctly protects only flexible easy/long volume and refuses to silently rescale prescriptive quality sessions (lines 144-181). | Taper correctly retains intensity while cutting volume: PHASE_DISTRIBUTIONS taper rows (phase_calculator.py:180-209) keep tempo at 0.10-0.12 and only zero out intervals/hills, while _get_taper_curve drops volume to 50-55% — this matches the 'volume down, intensity retained' best practice. | Peak mileage is bounded by multiple sane guards: per-distance multipliers, an absolute MAX_PEAK_MILEAGE ceiling, ACWR injury-risk reduction (15-25%), a volume-trend-aware week-over-week cap, and a distributable-volume cap (mileage_progression.py:363-373) so low-run-count plans don't force individual runs past structural limits. | Distance-aware periodization is real: marathon gets a longer build and 3-week taper, 5K gets more peak/sharpening and a 1-week taper, and trail/ultra brackets get bespoke phase splits and aggressive tapers (eccentric-damage rationale).


### [HIGH/methodology-gap] Plans of 12 weeks or shorter get ZERO recovery/deload weeks
- **loc:** app/core/training/phase_calculator.py:397-404 (is_recovery_week); confirmed via calculate_phases for 6/8/12-wk plans
- **evidence:** is_recovery_week gates deloads on `phase_length < 4` per phase. For weeks<=12 the base/build phases are 2-4 weeks each, so NO recovery week is ever placed. Verified: 12-wk Marathon {base:3,build:4,peak:2,taper:3}, 12-wk Half/5K/10K, and all 6-8 wk plans return recovery_weeks=[]. A 12-week marathon block ramps for 9 straight loading weeks with no deload before a 3-week taper.
- **impact:** The 3-4 week deload cadence is a core periodization norm (and a Runna/RunMotion hallmark). Most real users pick 8-12 week plans, so the single most common plan length runs with no absorption window — accumulating fatigue and injury risk, the opposite of the safety the high-water machinery is designed to provide.
- **rec:** Decouple deload cadence from per-phase boundaries: track a global week counter and insert a recovery week roughly every 3-4 loading weeks regardless of phase, or lower the gate so 8-12 week plans get at least one mid-plan deload. At minimum place one deload before the final build/peak push on any plan >=8 weeks.
- **VERDICT:** confirmed (high) — Verified by executing calculate_phases + is_recovery_week for all 6/8/12-wk plans across 5K/10K/Half/Marathon: every one returns recovery_weeks=[]. The cited 12-wk Marathon {base:3,build:4,peak:2,taper:3} matches exactly. is_recovery_week (phase_calculator.py:397-403) gates non-peak deloads on phase_length>=4 AND week_in_phase%4==0; for <=12-wk plans no base/build phase is long enough to ever fire (see finding 2 for why even 4-week phases fail). Peak deload requires peak>=4 weeks (line 391), never met on short plans. So a 12-wk marathon loads ~9 weeks straight before a 3-wk taper with no absorption window. The impact framing (3-4wk deload cadence is a core periodization/Runna-RunMotion norm, and 8-12wk are the most common lengths) is sound coaching judgment.

### [MEDIUM/correctness] 4-week phases never get a recovery week despite the gate and docstring claiming they should
- **loc:** app/core/training/phase_calculator.py:398-403
- **evidence:** The gate rejects only `phase_length < 4`, so a 4-week phase is allowed through, but the placement condition is `week_in_phase > 0 and week_in_phase % 4 == 0`. With phase_start_week mapping week_in_phase to 0,1,2,3 for a 4-week phase, it never reaches 4, so no recovery fires. Verified: a 4-week base phase yields is_recovery_week=False for all weeks; only a 5+ week phase places one (on its 5th week). The docstring says 'only if the phase is long enough (>=4 weeks)'.
- **impact:** An off-by-one that silently raises the effective threshold from 4 to 5 weeks, making deloads even sparser than intended and contradicting the documented behavior. E.g. a 16-wk Half {base:6,build:6} only deloads at weeks 5 and 11, leaving the entire build's first 4 weeks plus peak with one deload.
- **rec:** Either change the gate to `phase_length < 5` to match actual behavior, or fix placement so a 4-week phase deloads on its 4th week (e.g. recover when week_in_phase reaches the phase length for short phases). Align code and docstring.
- **VERDICT:** unverified (low) —

### [MEDIUM/methodology-gap] High-base marathon runners get a base phase that detrains them for ~4-5 weeks
- **loc:** app/core/training/mileage_progression.py:393-407 with base_end_target = peak*0.70 (tuning.py:33)
- **evidence:** Base ramps current_km -> 0.70*peak. When current_km already exceeds 0.70*peak (a 70 km/wk runner on an 85 km peak: base target = 59.5), the linear ramp slopes DOWNWARD. Verified: Marathon 16-wk current=70 -> [67.4,64.8,62.1,59.5,...] then build climbs back. The first 4 weeks of base shed ~11 km/wk of established volume.

- **rec:** Floor the base_end_target at current_km (or progress from current_km upward) when current_km already exceeds peak*BASE_PHASE_END_FRACTION, so base never prescribes detraining for runners who arrive with sufficient volume.

## INTENSITY — Intensity distribution & workout design (core/training: workout_distribution, distribution_validator, quality_scorer, quality_caps, workout_builders, workout_registry, key_workout_library, workout_steps)

**How it works:** Per-week intensity is decided in two layers. First, get_workout_distribution (app/core/training/workout_distribution.py) sets a COUNT of quality sessions by phase and max_runs (base: 0-1, build: 1 early then up to 2, peak: 1-2, recovery/taper: 0) and dispatches to per-race-profile builders (_quality_for_road_5k/10k/half/marathon, trail variants) that name the quality types (interval/tempo/hill). validate_polarized_ratio (distribution_validator.py) then checks a COUNT-based hard ratio = (interval+tempo+hill)/(all runs incl. long) against HARD_TARGETS_ROAD/TRAIL (build 0.20, peak 0.25) and adds/removes one quality slot if it drifts beyond ±thresholds. Second, the VOLUME split comes from PHASE_DISTRIBUTIONS in phase_calculator.py via calculate_quality_distances/get_phase_distribution, which assigns each quality type a fraction of weekly km; quality_caps.py then bounds each quality session to ~85% of the long run and distance-scaled physiological ceilings, and resolve_low_budget_quality floors or demotes thin quality doses. Build/peak interval/tempo/hill/long slots are then overlaid with curated key workouts (key_workout_library/selection.py overlay_key_workout → builders.py build_key_workout_steps), which produce explicit structured rep/recovery steps with VDOT paces (workout_steps/quality.py, key_workout_builders.py) and distance-driven prose (rewrites.py). quality_scorer.py separately grades a logged run vs the planned type by effort range and ±8% pace tolerance. Key-workout selection rotates candidates by week_in_phase % len(candidates).

**Strengths:** Genuine Runna/RunMotion-grade structured sessions: key_workout_builders.py emits explicit warm-up, N x rep with distance/duration, recovery block, and cool-down steps each carrying pace_zone + pace_str, and rewrites.py regenerates the prose/structure from the actual assigned distance so the card, steps, and description stay numerically consistent (a real strength over prose-only plans). | Two independent intensity guards: a count-based polarized check (distribution_validator) plus a volume-based phase split (PHASE_DISTRIBUTIONS) with physiological per-session caps (quality_caps.QUALITY_CAPS_BY_DISTANCE and the 85%-of-long-run ceiling), which bounds hard load on both axes. | Race-specificity is modeled: 5K leans VO2max/intervals, marathon peak drops intervals for tempo/MP work (_quality_for_road_marathon), and curated key workouts progress toward goal-race pace (e.g. half_race_pace_segments, 10k_goal_pace_segments, marathon_mp_cutdown). | Quality-dose realism: resolve_low_budget_quality + QUALITY_MIN_DOSE_KM avoid scheduling token sub-dose quality days, demoting them to easy when the week can't afford a meaningful main set. | Profile-aware nudges (workout_distribution lines 80-91, 333-353) reduce quality when a runner already does >30% hard work and seed tempo before intervals for runners with no speed history.


### [HIGH/methodology-gap] Taper retains zero intensity — quality sessions are dropped entirely
- **loc:** app/core/training/workout_distribution.py:74-77 (else branch) and app/core/training/phase_calculator.py:180-209
- **evidence:** get_workout_distribution only assigns quality_workouts for phases 'base', 'build', 'peak' (and max_runs<=2 build/peak). The taper phase falls into the final `else: quality_workouts = 0`, so a taper week is scheduled as long + easy only. overlay_key_workout also returns early for any phase not in ('build','peak') (selection.py:179). Meanwhile PHASE_DISTRIBUTIONS['taper'] still allocates 10-12% of volume to 'tempo' (phase_calculator.py:183,189,197,203), but with no tempo slot scheduled that allocation is never realized.
- **impact:** A correct taper cuts VOLUME while RETAINING short, sharp intensity (e.g. a few race-pace reps or strides) to keep neuromuscular readiness. Dropping all quality for 1-3 weeks before race day leaves runners flat/under-sharpened and is the opposite of Runna/RunMotion taper design. The dead taper tempo% in the table also signals the two intensity layers are out of sync.
- **rec:** Add a taper branch that keeps 1 short quality slot (reduced volume, race-pace or strides), e.g. `elif phase == 'taper': quality_workouts = 1 if max_runs >= 3 else 0`, and allow overlay_key_workout (or a dedicated sharpener) to fire in taper with shortened reps. Align the taper PHASE_DISTRIBUTIONS tempo% with whatever slot is actually scheduled.
- **VERDICT:** partial (high) — The code mechanism is confirmed: get_workout_distribution only sets quality_workouts>0 for is_recovery_week=False with phase in base/build/peak (or max_runs<=2 build/peak). Taper is not in any of those branches, so for max_runs>2 it falls into `else: quality_workouts = 0` (line 76-77). overlay_key_workout returns early when phase not in (build,peak) (selection.py:180), and get_for_phase returns None for non-build/peak (line 294-295). PHASE_DISTRIBUTIONS['taper'] does allocate 10-12% to 'tempo' (phase_calculator.py:184,190,197,203) while no tempo slot is scheduled — the dead-allocation observation is real. So 'no scheduled quality session in taper' is CONFIRMED. The verdict is partial only because the impact overstates 'zero intensity / leaves runners flat': easy-run variant_idx==1 days still append 6x100m strides (workout_builders.py:139-165 build_easy_steps with_strides), so some neuromuscular stimulus survives incidentally — but it is not a guaranteed/scheduled sharpening element and is not race-pace specific. The methodology critique (a real taper retains short sharp intensity) is sound; the literal 'zero intensity' framing is slightly too strong.

### [HIGH/methodology-gap] Polarized 80/20 is enforced by session COUNT, not volume, and the long run is lumped with easy
- **loc:** app/core/training/distribution_validator.py:39-50 and tuning.py:227-228
- **evidence:** validate_polarized_ratio computes hard_pct = (interval+tempo+hill count) / (hard + easy + long count). The long run is counted as an easy/aerobic session in the denominator, and each session counts equally regardless of km. On a 4-run build week (1 long, 1 quality, 2 easy) hard_pct = 1/4 = 0.25 vs build target 0.20 + 0.05 excess = 0.25 (not strictly greater, so no correction). The actual 80/20 polarized model is defined by TIME/volume at intensity, not session count.
- **impact:** Count-based ratios systematically misrepresent intensity for low-run-count weeks (the common recreational case) and let a single 8-12km threshold/interval session pass as '20-25% hard' even when its hard km share is much smaller or larger than 20%. It cannot detect that a week with one big quality session and small easy runs is actually over-polarized toward hard volume. This is the weakest part of the polarized claim vs best-in-class apps that track time-in-zone.
- **rec:** Compute hard fraction by quality km / total km (the data is already available via quality_distances and long_run_distance) and validate against a volume target (~0.15-0.20). Keep the count check as a secondary structural guard. Exclude the long run from the easy denominator only after deciding the metric, but prefer a km-based numerator/denominator.
- **VERDICT:** unverified (low) — 

### [MEDIUM/correctness] Hills are double-mislabeled: scored at T-pace but built/effort-cued as R-pace (anaerobic) sprints
- **loc:** app/core/training/quality_scorer.py:22-29 (WORKOUT_PACE_ZONE hill->T, EXPECTED_EFFORT hill 7-8) vs app/core/training/workout_steps/quality.py:375-401 (build_hill_steps uses pace_zone='R') and workout_builders.py:307-331
- **evidence:** quality_scorer maps hill -> 'T' (threshold) pace zone with expected effort 7-8, but the actual hill step builder build_hill_steps prescribes '10 x 30 s hill, pace_zone=R, effort=hard uphill' (R = repetition/anaerobic), and the low-base interval hill variant also uses R (quality.py:344). The generic generate_hill_workout description text says 'steep hill repeats' / 'explosive bounds'. So the planned session is a short anaerobic/neuromuscular hill sprint, but the scorer judges the logged run against threshold pace.
- **impact:** When a runner logs a hill session, the quality score compares their average pace to T-pace, which is meaningless for 30s uphill sprints (pace on a hill is already unreliable, and T is the wrong target zone). - **rec:** Make the planned hill workout and the scorer agree: either treat short hill reps as effort-only (drop the pace component for hill in quality_scorer, weight effort 100%) or, if hills are intended as threshold climbs, change build_hill_steps to longer reps at T. Pick one hill stimulus and align EXPECTED_EFFORT, WORKOUT_PACE_ZONE, and the step builder.

## PACING — VDOT, pacing, zones &amp; long run

**How it works:** VDOT is computed in app/core/training/vdot_calculator.py from a single race (distance, time) using Daniels' VO2 cost polynomial (_vo2_at_velocity) divided by the time-based %VO2max curve (_pct_vo2max_at_time), then clamped to 25-85 and rounded to 1 dp; spot checks match published values (5K 19:57 -> VDOT 50.0). Pace zones are derived by inverting the VO2 polynomial at fixed %VO2max midpoints (E_slow 0.65 .. R 1.05) in get_pace_zones, plus easy sub-zones (recovery/easy/long_run) and predicted 5K/10K/target race paces from race_predictor.predict_time_for_distance (a binary search over the same equations, with piecewise grade, trail-inexperience and ultra-decay multipliers). zone_calculator.calculate_zones maps the VDOT E/M/T/I paces into a 5-band display table (recovery/aerobic/tempo/vo2max/race), anchoring zone 5 to goal pace when present or to marathon pace otherwise, and attaches HR bands from hr_zone_calculator.TRAINING_ZONE_HR_PERCENTAGES. hr_zone_calculator separately builds a personal max-HR zone model (ZONE_DEFINITIONS) and maps workout types to zones. long_run_calculator.calculate_long_run_distance multiplies weekly volume by a phase/distance-progressed ratio (tuning.ROAD_LONG_RUN_RATIOS), then bounds it with experience-tiered caps (ROAD_LONG_RUN_CAPS), a volume-aware scale toward a hard ceiling, and floors; in practice long runs land at ~27-42% of weekly volume. workout_steps/aerobic.py and metrics.py build step lists with paces drawn from the zone strings and parse those strings back to min/km for distance accounting.

**Strengths:** VDOT core math is faithful to Daniels: the VO2/%VO2max formulas reproduce textbook VDOT values (5K 19:57 -> 50.0, 10K 40:00 -> 51.9) and the binary-search race predictor is internally consistent with the VDOT used to derive it. | Sensible data-quality guards: calculate_vdot rejects sub-2:30/km efforts (GPS artifacts) and hilly runs (>20 m/km) that would pollute a flat-ground estimate, and clamps to a realistic 25-85 range. | Long-run sizing is genuinely well-bounded: phase- and distance-aware ratio progression plus experience-tiered absolute caps and a weekly-share cap keep single long runs around 30-42% of weekly volume, close to the 30-35% guideline, with a gentle week-1 nudge against jarring jumps from the runner's historical max. | Race prediction is thoughtfully extended beyond flat road: piecewise grade penalties, a trail-inexperience multiplier, and an ultra-endurance decay address the known places where raw VDOT over-predicts. | Pace-string parsing (metrics._parse_pace_str_to_min_per_km) correctly handles both en-dash and hyphen ranges and deliberately takes the slower end for conservative distance estimates, with documented default-pace fallbacks.

## ADAPT-SIGNALS — Adaptation signals & recommendation logic

**How it works:** `gather_signals` (app/contexts/plan/adaptation/plan_adjuster.py:63) loads all RunLogs for a plan, RunFeedback, last-14-day ReadinessLogs, VDOT trend and a 42-day TRIMP-style training-load summary, then calls `compute_adjustment_signals` (signal_computer/__init__.py:127). Six per-signal computers (signal_computer/signals.py) each return a SignalContribution: volume (recency- and importance-weighted actual/planned km ratio with per-type Bayesian shrinkage), effort (recency-weighted perceived effort → factor 0.85–1.10, plus a trend modifier and an effort_quality_score drift modifier), HR-zone (avg zone deviation → 0.90–1.05), feedback (warning/positive sentiment ratios → 0.92–1.05), readiness (self-reported logs if ≥3, else a real TSB-derived fallback factor 0.95–1.03), and completion (weighted completed/scheduled → 0.90–1.05). Factors are combined as a phase-weighted linear sum (PHASE_WEIGHTS in tuning.py), multiplied by a mountain factor, then passed through `apply_clamps` (clamps.py) which applies overreach (volume×effort, solo-effort, HR, race-effort), VDOT-decline, and TSB-form clamps before a final hysteresis-banded clamp (standard ±15%, expanded -30/+25% after 3 consecutive same-direction moves or a primed peak TSB). `evaluate_weekly_recommendation` (recommendation_evaluator.py:37) is invoked per-run / on plan view, gated to once per completed ISO-week via `last_recommendation_week`; it parks a recommendation or auto-applies it (auto_adjust_enabled), skipping moves under 2% and small reversals (<5%). VDOT recalibration (vdot_recalibrator.py) rewrites future pace zones when best-recent VDOT drifts ≥1.0 from the plan VDOT. Distance changes are applied in week_adjuster.py with per-type and per-workout ceilings plus a 10%/week growth cap and long-run dominance cap (safety.py).

**Strengths:** TSB/form is a genuine CTL/ATL EWMA model (training_load_service.py:98-141), not a heuristic — CTL is a 42-day and ATL a 7-day EWMA of TRIMP-style daily load, TSB uses yesterday's CTL-ATL per TrainingPeaks convention, with a 14-day warm-up sweep before the window. Readiness now falls back to this real TSB proxy when self-reports are sparse (signals.py:285-317), so the signal contributes objective recovery info for the majority of users. | Safety layering is strong and multi-tiered: overreach clamps (volume+effort, solo-effort hold, HR adherence, recent race-effort, TSB<=-25), an OVERREACH_OVERRIDE_CLAMP that forces 'reduce or hold' so a strong volume signal can't show an 'increase' banner alongside an overreach alert (signal_computer/__init__.py:216), plus per-type clamps (0.85-1.15), a per-workout WORKOUT_CEILING (1.25x baseline), long-run floor protection, and a 10%/week growth cap reusing the generation-time WEEK_OVER_WEEK_CAP (safety.py:147). | Hysteresis is thoughtfully designed: a dead-band (<2% no-op), a small-reversal guard that holds steady when a sub-5% move would flip the last applied direction (recommendation_evaluator.py:504), and an expanded clamp range only unlocked after 3 sustained same-direction moves. This directly targets week-over-week wobble that erodes trust. | Bayesian shrinkage on per-type volume ratios (signals.py:84-91) sensibly blends a sparse per-type ratio toward the overall volume ratio with confidence = min(1, n_runs*0.30), avoiding overreaction to one logged tempo. | Recency weighting via a principled exponential half-life (3 weeks, RECENCY_HALF_LIFE_WEEKS) is applied consistently across volume, effort, HR, feedback and completion, so recent weeks dominate the signal as best practice suggests. | Centralized, well-documented tuning surface (tuning.py) makes thresholds auditable and tunable in one place, and a backtest harness (per project memory) already surfaced and fixed real tuning gaps (readiness TSB proxy, solo-effort clamp, distance-0 completion skip).

## LOAD-READINESS — Fatigue/load modeling & readiness

**How it works:** TrainingLoadService (app/contexts/runner/fitness/training_load_service.py) computes a per-run TRIMP-style load = duration_minutes * intensity, where intensity comes from perceived_effort (0.5 + RPE/10*1.5), else avg_heart_rate/150, else a flat 1.0 (lines 40-53). It buckets runs into daily loads, then sweeps day-by-day from sweep_start (min(first_run_date, window_start) - 14 days, lines 94-96) to today, advancing two EWMAs initialized at 0: CTL = ctl + (load-ctl)/42 and ATL = atl + (load-atl)/7 (lines 98-113), and recording TSB = CTL-ATL using yesterday's values (correct TrainingPeaks convention, line 109). It separately computes a coupled-rolling-average ACWR over a 7-day acute / 28-day chronic window (lines 118-128) and classifies risk/form bands (lines 155-173). This is NOT display-only: plan_adjuster.py calls get_training_load(lookback_days=42) (line 164) and the current TSB feeds both a hard clamp in adaptation/clamps.py (TSB<=-25 caps the weekly multiplier at 0.92, lines 105-117) and a mild readiness factor in signal_computer/signals.py (_readiness_factor_from_tsb, lines 269-303) used as a fallback when the user has no subjective ReadinessLog entries. Separately, ReadinessService (readiness_service.py) builds a race-readiness report from five weighted components — volume(25), vdot(25), long_run(20), consistency(15), taper(15) — none of which reference CTL/ATL/TSB/ACWR (readiness_scoring.py). performance_service.py and fitness_service.py are plan-creation services that derive pace/VDOT/weekly-km and max-HR from run logs but do not use the fatigue model.

**Strengths:** TSB is computed with the correct convention (yesterday's CTL-ATL, before applying today's load, line 109) so it reflects freshness going into the day rather than after it. | The fatigue model is genuinely wired into adaptation, not display-only: extreme overreaching (TSB<=-25) clamps the weekly progression multiplier, and TSB also serves as an objective fallback readiness signal when users never self-report (signals.py lines 296-303), which is the realistic common case. | EWMA is swept over the runner's full logged history regardless of the lookback window, so for long-history runners CTL/ATL warm up correctly rather than being recomputed from a truncated window each call. | Sensible 3-tier max-HR fallback (run-data 98th percentile -> age formula -> pace heuristic) with explicit confidence labels (performance_service.py lines 78-142). | ACWR risk bands (0.8/1.3/1.5) and TSB form bands match accepted sports-science thresholds, and the clamp values are externalized in tuning.py for tunability.

## COACHING — Coaching feedback & guidance quality

**How it works:** Per-run feedback is produced by CoachingFeedbackEngine.generate_feedback (app/core/coaching/coaching_feedback_engine.py), which fans out to pure sub-generators — pace_feedback, hr_zone_feedback, an _effort_feedback wrapper around calculate_quality_score, volume_feedback, and pattern_feedback — then determine_sentiment (sentiment_classifier.py) classifies the aggregate into positive/warning/info by substring-matching the generated prose. FeedbackService.generate_and_store (app/contexts/runner/fitness/feedback_service.py) persists these as a RunFeedback row, and get_weekly_feedback_summary aggregates them into weekly highlights by re-parsing the stored prose for keywords ("fast", "slow", "behind", "ahead", "too hard"). Plan-time per-session rationale comes from generate_coaching_note (coaching_notes_generator.py), a static dict of prose templates keyed by (workout_type, phase) with type-only fallbacks, written onto each workout as coaching_rationale by the generators. The Today-tab "Coach's Note" is assembled by coach_narrative_service.build_coach_note: it builds a deterministic fact pack (build_coach_summary/build_today/build_training_age in coach_summary_service.py), picks at most one signal-driven "focus" via recognition.select_today_focus, and either voices it through an injected AI CoachNarrator or falls back to recognition.build_fallback_note. training_tips.get_tips_for_week selects four rotating tips per week from a large static database, and verdict.verdict_from_thresholds backs the gap-analysis ladder (used only in gap_metrics.py, not in narrative coaching).

**Strengths:** The Coach's Note focus selector (recognition.select_today_focus) is genuinely directive and well-prioritized: safety/overreach first, then low readiness, then execution drift, then effort-creep, then a 'primed to push' cue, and it deliberately returns None on most days so the note never nags — this matches the user's stated preference for signal-grounded, non-affirmational coaching. | Signals feeding the note are real and multi-dimensional (TSB/CTL/ATL form, overreach, effort trend, VDOT trend, morning readiness, recency-weighted pace patterns), so the lead guidance is grounded in the runner's own data rather than generic templates. | pattern_analyzer.pattern_feedback uses a 14-day half-life recency weighting plus a consecutive-streak trigger and is correctly direction-aware (warns about easy/long run being too fast, tempo/interval being too slow), which is a sound way to surface habitual mis-pacing without overreacting to one run. | Recognition chips (build_recognition) carefully keep lifetime journey stats and plan-week framing as separate chips and compute the hard numbers in Python rather than trusting model prose, avoiding a common AI-coaching hallucination failure mode. | calculate_quality_score sensibly down-weights pace on hills (50/50 vs 40/60) because GPS pace is unreliable on inclines, and the (workout_type, phase) coaching notes are physiologically literate and phase-appropriate.
