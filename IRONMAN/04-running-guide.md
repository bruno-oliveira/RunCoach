# Complete Running Guide for Triathlon

## Why Running Matters

Running is the final discipline and often where races are won or lost. It's also where fatigue from the swim and bike accumulates. Training to run efficiently off the bike—while managing effort from the previous hours—is the unique challenge of triathlon running.

## The Unique Nature of Triathlon Running

Running after swimming and cycling is different from fresh running:
- Legs feel "heavy" or "dead" initially
- Cardiovascular system is already fatigued
- Muscles used differently after cycling
- Mental fatigue from hours of focus
- Nutrition plays a larger role in performance

## Running Form for Triathletes

### Key Elements

#### Cadence
- **Optimal:** 170-180 steps per minute
- **Higher cadence:** Reduces impact forces
- **Lower cadence:** Overstriding, braking forces
- **How to check:** Count footstrikes for 15 seconds, multiply by 4

#### Foot Strike
- **Midfoot:** Most efficient for most runners
- **Heel strike:** Common but can increase braking forces
- **Forefoot:** Fast but higher calf/Achilles load
- **Key:** Land under your center of mass

#### Body Position
- **Torso:** Slight forward lean from ankles
- **Head:** Eyes forward, not looking down
- **Arms:** 90-degree bend, swing forward/back (not crossing)
- **Hands:** Relaxed, not clenched
- **Shoulders:** Low and relaxed

#### Stride Length
- Natural stride length for your body
- Don't overstride (reaching with foot)
- Power comes from push-off, not reach

### Form Cues to Practice
- "Run tall" (posture)
- "Light steps" (impact)
- "Quick feet" (cadence)
- "Relax shoulders" (tension)
- "Eyes on horizon" (head position)

## Running Drills

### Purpose of Drills
- Improve neuromuscular coordination
- Reinforce proper mechanics
- Warm-up activation

### Key Drills

#### High Knees
- Run in place with exaggerated knee lift
- Quick ground contact
- Knees to hip height
- 2-3 sets x 20 seconds

#### Butt Kicks
- Heels to glutes
- Quick cadence
- Minimal forward movement
- 2-3 sets x 20 seconds

#### Strides
- Accelerate to near-max pace over 80-100m
- Hold briefly, then decelerate
- Focus on good form at speed
- 4-6 repetitions after easy runs

#### Bounding
- Exaggerated running motion
- Long, powerful strides
- Focus on push-off
- 3-4 sets x 30m

#### Single-Leg Hops
- Hop on one leg, focus on quick ground contact
- Builds stability and power
- 2 sets x 20m each leg

## Training Types

### Easy Runs
**Purpose:** Build aerobic base, recovery
- Intensity: 65-75% max HR (conversational pace)
- Duration: 20-60 minutes
- Frequency: Most runs are easy runs
- Terrain: Flat to rolling

### Tempo/Threshold Runs
**Purpose:** Improve sustainable pace (lactate threshold)
- Intensity: 80-90% max HR ("comfortably hard")
- Duration: 20-40 minutes continuous or broken
- Frequency: Once per week typically
- Pace: 10K pace + 15-20 sec/mile

### Interval Sessions
**Purpose:** Increase VO2max and speed

#### Short Intervals
- Duration: 200-400m (30-90 seconds)
- Intensity: Near maximum
- Recovery: Equal or greater time
- Reps: 8-16

#### Longer Intervals
- Duration: 800m-1600m (3-6 minutes)
- Intensity: 5K-10K pace
- Recovery: 1-2 minutes
- Reps: 4-8

#### Fartlek
- Unstructured speed play
- Random bursts of speed
- Natural terrain (surge to tree, then recover)
- Good for base phase

### Long Runs
**Purpose:** Build endurance and metabolic efficiency
- Duration: 60 minutes to 2+ hours (distance dependent)
- Intensity: Easy to moderate
- Frequency: Once per week
- Progress: Build time gradually

### Brick Runs
**Purpose:** Adapt body to running off the bike
- Duration: 10-60 minutes after cycling
- Intensity: Varies by session purpose
- Frequency: At least once per week

## Sample Training Weeks

### Beginner/Sprint
```
Monday: Rest or cross-train
Tuesday: 30 min easy + 4 strides
Wednesday: Bike + 10 min brick run
Thursday: 25 min easy
Friday: Rest
Saturday: Long bike + 15 min brick run
Sunday: 45 min long run (easy)
```

### Intermediate/Olympic
```
Monday: Rest
Tuesday: 45 min easy + 6 strides
Wednesday: Tempo run: 10 min WU, 20 min tempo, 10 min CD
Thursday: 40 min easy
Friday: Bike + 20 min brick run
Saturday: Long bike + 20 min brick run
Sunday: 60 min long run
```

### Advanced/70.3
```
Monday: Rest or easy swim
Tuesday: Intervals: 10 min WU, 5 x 800m @ 5K pace, 2 min RI, 10 min CD
Wednesday: 50 min easy
Thursday: Tempo: 10 min WU, 30 min tempo, 10 min CD
Friday: Bike + 30 min brick run
Saturday: Long bike + 30 min brick run
Sunday: 75-90 min long run (progressive effort)
```

## The Brick Workout

### What is a Brick?
A brick workout combines two disciplines back-to-back, typically bike-to-run. Named because your legs feel like "bricks" when you start running.

### Why Brick Workouts Matter
1. **Neuromuscular adaptation:** Body learns to switch from pedaling to running
2. **Mental preparation:** Experience the discomfort before race day
3. **Practice T2:** Smooth transitions become automatic
4. **Nutrition testing:** Test race-day fueling strategy

### Types of Brick Sessions

#### Short Transition Bricks
- 60-90 min bike
- 5-15 min run immediately after
- Focus: Smooth transition, not fitness

#### Medium Bricks
- 2-3 hour bike
- 30-45 min run
- Focus: Endurance and pacing

#### Long Bricks (70.3 and longer)
- 3-4 hour bike
- 45-90 min run
- Focus: Race simulation, nutrition practice

#### Race Simulation Bricks
- Race-pace bike segment
- Race-pace run segment
- Full nutrition practice
- Full gear practice

### Transition Practice
- Set up transition area at home
- Practice bike-to-run 10-20 times
- Timing reveals inefficiencies
- Video analysis helpful

## Running Pacing by Distance

### Sprint Triathlon (5K)
- Pace: Similar to standalone 5K or slightly slower
- Strategy: Push from the start, little held back

### Olympic Triathlon (10K)
- Pace: 10-20 seconds slower per mile than standalone 10K
- Strategy: Negative split; conservative first half

### Quarter Ironman (10.5K)
- Pace: Similar to standalone 10K pace minus 15-25 sec/mile
- Strategy: Very even pacing throughout

### Half Ironman (13.1 miles)
- Pace: 30-60 seconds slower than standalone half marathon
- Strategy: Start deliberately slow; build through the run

## Running Off the Bike

### The First 5-10 Minutes
- Legs feel heavy/dead (normal!)
- Cardiovascular adjustment happening
- Focus on cadence, not pace
- Resist urge to go too fast

### Finding Rhythm
- Pace settles after adjustment
- Heart rate may be elevated initially
- Effort may feel hard while pace is moderate
- Give it 10 minutes before assessing

### Strategies for Success
1. **Start with shorter strides:** Quick turnover helps
2. **Ignore pace initially:** Focus on effort
3. **Stay patient:** First mile is deceptive
4. **Nutrition matters:** Proper bike fueling critical
5. **Practice regularly:** Body adapts over time

## Trail and Terrain Training

### Benefits of Trail Running
- Builds ankle and foot strength
- Reduces impact forces
- Mental break from road running
- Improves balance and coordination

### Hill Running

#### Uphill Technique
- Shorten stride, maintain cadence
- Slight forward lean
- Drive arms
- Look ahead, not at feet
- Power walk if needed

#### Downhill Technique
- Short, quick steps
- Don't brake excessively
- Relax and let gravity assist
- Save leg energy

#### Hill Workouts

**Long Hills:**
- Find 3-5 minute hill
- 4-6 repetitions at threshold
- Easy jog down recovery

**Short Hills:**
- Find 45-90 second hill
- 8-12 repetitions at high intensity
- Full walk-down recovery

**Rolling Route:**
- Continuous run with ups and downs
- Push on some hills, recover on others

## Injury Prevention

### Most Common Running Injuries
- Runner's knee (patellofemoral pain)
- IT band syndrome
- Achilles tendinitis
- Plantar fasciitis
- Shin splints
- Hamstring strain

### Prevention Strategies

#### Training Factors
- Build gradually (10% rule for volume increase)
- Include recovery weeks
- Vary running surfaces
- Don't ignore rest days
- Listen to warning signs

#### Strength Factors
- Hip strength and stability
- Core strength
- Single-leg balance
- Ankle mobility

#### Equipment Factors
- Proper shoe fit
- Appropriate shoe age (300-500 miles max)
- Replace shoes before they're worn out
- Orthotics if needed

### Warning Signs (Stop Training)
- Sharp pain (not dull ache)
- Pain that changes gait
- Pain that worsens during run
- Swelling around joint
- Pain that persists after run

### Warning Signs (Modify Training)
- Dull ache that started recently
- Stiffness that improves with warm-up
- Minor soreness after hard workout
- General fatigue

### Return from Injury
- Reduce volume significantly
- Start with run/walk
- Build back gradually
- Address root cause
- Consider physical therapy

## Running Nutrition

### Before Running
- Light meal 2-3 hours before
- Small snack 30-60 minutes before if needed
- Hydration is ongoing process
- Avoid high-fiber foods before runs

### During Running
- Sprint: No nutrition needed
- Olympic: Water/electrolyte drink in hot conditions
- 70.3: 30-60g carbs per hour on course
- Practice with race-day products

### After Running
- Protein + carbs within 30-60 minutes
- Continue hydration
- Anti-inflammatory foods help recovery

## Heart Rate and Power for Running

### Heart Rate Zones

| Zone | % Max HR | Effort | Use |
|------|----------|--------|-----|
| 1 | 50-60% | Very easy | Recovery |
| 2 | 60-70% | Easy | Aerobic base |
| 3 | 70-80% | Moderate | Aerobic endurance |
| 4 | 80-90% | Hard | Threshold |
| 5 | 90-100% | Maximum | VO2max |

### Running Power (if using power meter)
- Similar principles to cycling power
- Watts/kg often used
- Power is less affected by external factors than pace
- Useful for pacing on variable terrain

### Pace Zones
- Use recent race times or time trials
- VDOT calculator (Jack Daniels)
- Training paces derived from race pace

## Race Day Running Strategy

### Starting the Run
- Transition: Don't rush T2
- First 0.5-1 mile: Controlled, find rhythm
- Ignore pace watch initially
- Focus on form cues

### Mid-Run
- Settle into planned pace/effort
- Take nutrition at aid stations
- Use form cues when struggling
- Break into segments mentally

### Closing the Run
- Last 1-2 miles: Increase effort if possible
- Save something for finishing kick
- Mental focus on finish line
- Strong finish regardless of time

### Common Race Day Mistakes
- Starting too fast after transition
- Ignoring nutrition needs
- Panicking when pace drops
- Giving up mentally
