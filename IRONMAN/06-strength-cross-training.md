# Strength Training and Cross-Training Guide

## Why Strength Training Matters for Triathletes

Triathlon is an endurance sport, so why lift weights? Strength training addresses what swim-bike-run cannot:

- **Injury prevention:** Build resilience in muscles, tendons, and ligaments
- **Performance:** Increase force production and economy
- **Movement quality:** Correct imbalances from repetitive motions
- **Aging well:** Maintain muscle mass and bone density
- **Core stability:** Essential for all three sports

## Key Principles

### 1. Train Movement, Not Just Muscles
Triathlon requires coordinated movement. Focus on functional exercises that train muscle groups to work together.

### 2. Address Weaknesses
Every triathlete has imbalances from their training. Strength work should address these, not reinforce them.

### 3. Periodize Strength Training
Match your strength work to your sport training:
- **Base phase:** Building strength, more time in gym
- **Build phase:** Maintaining strength, less gym time
- **Peak phase:** Minimal strength work, sport focus
- **Taper:** Eliminate or very light strength work

### 4. Don't Sacrifice Key Sessions
Strength training supports endurance training, not replaces it. Don't lift before your most important swim/bike/run sessions.

## Core Training

### Why Core Matters
Core muscles stabilize your spine and transfer force between upper and lower body. Weak core = energy leaks and injury risk.

### Key Core Exercises

#### Plank Variations
- Standard plank: Hold 30-60 seconds
- Side plank: 30 seconds each side
- Plank with shoulder taps: 10 each side
- Plank with leg lifts: 10 each leg

#### Dead Bug
- Lying on back, arms and legs in air
- Lower opposite arm and leg toward ground
- Keep lower back flat
- 10-15 each side

#### Bird Dog
- Hands and knees position
- Extend opposite arm and opposite leg
- Keep spine neutral
- 10-15 each side

#### Pallof Press
- Band or cable at chest height
- Press away from chest, resist rotation
- Hold 2-3 seconds
- 10-15 each side

#### Russian Twists
- Sit with feet off ground, torso at 45 degrees
- Rotate side to side
- Add weight for progression
- 20 total reps

### Weekly Core Routine
- Perform 2-3 times per week
- Select 3-4 exercises
- 2-3 sets each

## Strength Training for Swimming

### Swimming-Specific Needs
- Upper body pulling strength
- Shoulder stability
- Core rotation power
- Lat and back strength

### Key Exercises

#### Pull-Ups/Lat Pull-Downs
- Primary back strengthener
- 3-4 sets x 8-12 reps
- Assisted if needed

#### Seated Cable Rows
- Horizontal pulling
- 3 sets x 10-15 reps

#### Band Pull-Aparts
- Shoulder health and posture
- 3 sets x 15-20 reps

#### Face Pulls
- Rear delts and rotator cuff
- 3 sets x 15-20 reps

#### Medicine Ball Throws
- Rotational power
- 3 sets x 8-10 each side

## Strength Training for Cycling

### Cycling-Specific Needs
- Lower body strength
- Unilateral (single-leg) stability
- Hip stability
- Core endurance for aero position

### Key Exercises

#### Squats (Back, Front, or Goblet)
- Primary leg strengthener
- 3-4 sets x 6-12 reps

#### Lunges (Forward, Reverse, Walking)
- Single-leg strength and balance
- 3 sets x 8-12 each leg

#### Single-Leg Deadlifts
- Hamstrings and hip stability
- 3 sets x 8-12 each leg

#### Step-Ups
- Single-leg strength
- 3 sets x 10-15 each leg

#### Hip Thrusts
- Glute strength
- 3 sets x 10-15 reps

#### Calf Raises
- Ankle stability and power
- 3 sets x 15-20 reps

## Strength Training for Running

### Running-Specific Needs
- Single-leg stability
- Hip strength and stability
- Ankle strength
- Core stability for upright posture

### Key Exercises

#### Single-Leg Squats
- Ultimate single-leg strength
- 3 sets x 6-10 each leg
- Use support if needed

#### Calf Raises (Single-Leg)
- Ankle stability and push-off power
- 3 sets x 10-15 each leg

#### Lateral Lunges
- Hip strength in side plane
- 3 sets x 8-12 each side

#### Clamshells
- Hip external rotation strength
- 3 sets x 15-20 each side

#### Single-Leg Glute Bridges
- Glute activation and strength
- 3 sets x 10-15 each leg

## Complete Strength Sessions

### Sample Session 1: Full Body (45-60 min)
```
Warm-up: 5-10 min light cardio + mobility

1. Squats/Goblet Squats: 3x10
2. Pull-Ups/Assisted Pull-Ups: 3x8
3. Romanian Deadlifts: 3x10
4. Push-Ups: 3x10-15
5. Lunges: 3x10 each leg
6. Plank: 3x30 sec
7. Band Pull-Aparts: 3x15
```

### Sample Session 2: Lower Body Focus (30-45 min)
```
Warm-up: 5 min + activation

1. Single-Leg Squats (assisted): 3x8 each
2. Single-Leg Deadlifts: 3x10 each
3. Hip Thrusts: 3x12
4. Lateral Lunges: 3x10 each side
5. Calf Raises: 3x15
6. Plank with leg lifts: 3x30 sec
```

### Sample Session 3: Upper Body + Core (30 min)
```
1. Pull-Ups/Lat Pull-Downs: 3x10
2. Push-Ups: 3x15
3. Seated Rows: 3x12
4. Overhead Press: 3x10
5. Dead Bug: 3x10 each side
6. Pallof Press: 3x10 each side
7. Plank: 3x45 sec
```

## Pliometric Training

### What Are Plyometrics?
Jumping, bounding, and explosive movements that train the stretch-shorten cycle—essential for running economy.

### Key Exercises

#### Box Jumps
- Jump onto box, land softly
- 3 sets x 6-8 reps

#### Jump Squats
- Squat down, explode up
- 3 sets x 10-12 reps

#### Bounding
- Exaggerated running strides
- 3 sets x 20 meters

#### Single-Leg Hops
- Quick hops on one leg
- 3 sets x 20m each leg

### When to Include Plyometrics
- Base and build phases
- Not during peak/taper
- After establishing base strength
- On fresh legs, not after hard runs

## Cross-Training Options

### Why Cross-Train?
- Cardiovascular fitness without impact
- Different movement patterns
- Mental variety
- Recovery or active rest tool

### Best Cross-Training Options

#### Elliptical
- Low impact, resembles running motion
- Good for injured athletes
- Can simulate hills and intensity

#### Rowing Ergometer
- Full body cardio
- Posture strengthening
- Good cross-training for swimming

#### Stair Climber
- Lower body focus
- Good for run conditioning
- Low impact

#### Hiking
- Low intensity endurance
- Mental break
- Trail running simulation

#### Stand-Up Paddleboarding
- Core and balance
- Upper body endurance
- Open water experience

#### Cross-Country Skiing
- Excellent full-body cardio
- Winter alternative
- Similar cardiovascular demands

### How to Incorporate Cross-Training

#### As Recovery
- Easy effort (65-70% max HR)
- 30-60 minutes
- Day after hard workout

#### As Volume Substitute
- Replace an easy run with cross-training
- Same duration as swim/bike/run would have been
- Maintain aerobic fitness while reducing impact

#### During Injury
- Maintain fitness without aggravating injury
- May need to increase volume if replacing running
- Work with physical therapist

## Flexibility and Mobility

### Why Flexibility Matters
- Full range of motion for efficiency
- Injury prevention
- Recovery enhancement
- Movement quality

### Key Areas for Triathletes

#### Hips
- Hip flexor stretches (kneeling lunge)
- Pigeon pose (external rotation)
- Butterfly stretch (adductors)

#### Shoulders
- Cross-body stretch (rear delt)
- Lat stretch (hanging or doorway)
- Cow-face arms (rotator cuff)

#### Thoracic Spine
- Cat-cow (flexion/extension)
- Thread the needle (rotation)
- Foam roller extension

#### Ankles/Calves
- Wall calf stretch (gastrocnemius)
- Bent-knee calf stretch (soleus)
- Ankle circles

#### Hamstrings
- Standing toe touch
- Seated hamstring stretch
- Lying hamstring stretch with strap

### Mobility Routine (15-20 min)

```
1. Cat-Cow: 10 reps
2. Thread the Needle: 10 each side
3. Hip Flexor Stretch: 60 sec each side
4. Pigeon Pose: 60 sec each side
5. Standing Hamstring: 60 sec each side
6. Calf Stretch: 60 sec each side
7. Lat Stretch: 60 sec each side
8. Chest Opener: 60 sec
9. Foam Roller (back/IT band): 2-3 min
```

### When to Stretch
- **Best:** After workouts, when muscles are warm
- **OK:** On rest days, as separate session
- **Avoid:** Before high-intensity sessions (dynamic warm-up preferred)

## Dynamic Warm-Up

### Before Every Workout

#### General Warm-Up (5-10 min)
- Light cardio (jog, spin, etc.)
- Movement prep exercises
- Sport-specific activation

#### Running Warm-Up Example
```
5 min easy jog
Leg swings (front-back and side): 10 each
Walking lunges: 10 each leg
High knees: 20 meters
Butt kicks: 20 meters
Strides (accelerations): 4 x 80m
```

#### Swimming Warm-Up Example
```
200 easy swim
4 x 25 drill (catch-up, single arm)
4 x 50 build (progressively faster)
100 easy
```

## Recovery Modalities

### Sleep
- Most important recovery tool
- 7-9 hours per night
- Quality and consistency matter
- Naps: 20-30 minutes if needed

### Nutrition
- Covered in nutrition guide
- Recovery window: 30-60 min post-workout
- Protein throughout the day

### Active Recovery
- Very light exercise (recovery ride, easy swim)
- 20-40 minutes
- Heart rate well below threshold

### Massage/Foam Rolling
- Self-massage: Foam roller, massage ball
- Professional massage: Every 2-4 weeks if possible
- Focus on tight/problem areas

### Compression
- Compression socks/tights
- May help with blood flow and recovery
- Wear post-workout or travel

### Cold Therapy
- Ice baths: 10-15 minutes post-workout
- Cold water immersion
- May reduce inflammation

### Heat Therapy
- Hot baths/showers
- Sauna sessions (with caution)
- Promotes blood flow

## Injury Prevention Screening

### Self-Assessments

#### Single-Leg Balance
- Stand on one leg, eyes closed
- Should be able to hold 30+ seconds
- Significantly different sides = concern

#### Overhead Squat
- Squat with arms overhead
- Watch for: Forward lean, knees collapsing in, arms dropping forward
- Indicates mobility or strength limitations

#### Single-Leg Squat
- Single-leg squat to 45 degrees
- Watch for: Knee valgus (collapsing inward), hip drop
- Indicates hip/glute weakness

### When to Seek Professional Help
- Persistent pain (>7 days)
- Pain affecting training
- Recurring issues
- Major asymmetries
- Pre-season screening

## Sample Weekly Strength Schedule

### In-Season (Build/Peak)
```
Monday: Rest
Tuesday: 20 min core + mobility
Wednesday: 30 min strength (injury prevention focus)
Thursday: Off
Friday: 15 min activation
Saturday: Long day - no strength
Sunday: 20 min mobility + core
```

### Off-Season/Base
```
Monday: Rest
Tuesday: 45 min full-body strength
Wednesday: Swim/Bike/Run + 20 min core
Thursday: 30 min strength (lower body focus)
Friday: Swim/Bike/Run + mobility
Saturday: 45 min strength (upper body focus)
Sunday: Long workout + 15 min mobility
```
