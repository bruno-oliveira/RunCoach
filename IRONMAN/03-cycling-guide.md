# Complete Cycling Guide for Triathlon

## Why Cycling Matters

Cycling represents 50-60% of race time in most triathlons. It's the longest segment and the one where equipment, aerodynamics, and pacing strategy have the biggest impact on your result. A well-executed bike leg sets you up for a strong run.

## The Unique Challenge of Triathlon Cycling

Unlike pure cycling events, triathlon cycling has a key constraint: you must run afterward. This changes everything:
- Power output must be sustainable
- Position must allow running muscles to recover
- Nutrition must be consumed while riding
- Pacing is critical to avoid "blowing up" on the run

## Bike Types for Triathlon

### Road Bike
- **Pros:** Versatile, comfortable, lower cost, good handling
- **Cons:** Less aerodynamic, not optimized for triathlon position
- **Best for:** Beginners, hilly courses, budget-conscious athletes

### Triathlon/TT Bike
- **Pros:** Aerodynamic, optimized for flat/fast courses, geometry suits running off the bike
- **Cons:** Expensive, poor handling, not legal for draft-legal races
- **Best for:** Flat courses, experienced cyclists, longer distances

### Making Your Choice
- First triathlon: Use what you have (road bike is fine)
- Committed to sport: Consider tri bike for 70.3 and longer
- Hilly course: Road bike may be faster
- Draft-legal race: Road bike required

## Bike Fit

### Why Fit Matters
- Injury prevention (knees, back, neck)
- Comfort for long rides
- Power optimization
- Aerodynamic efficiency

### Key Fit Elements

#### Saddle Height
- Heel on pedal at 6 o'clock should produce straight leg
- Knee bend should be 25-35 degrees at bottom of stroke
- Too high: Hips rock, power loss, injury risk
- Too low: Knee strain, reduced power

#### Saddle Position (Fore/Aft)
- Knee over pedal spindle (KOPS) method
- More forward for tri position (recruits different muscles)
- TT position: Front of knee 1-2cm ahead of spindle

#### Handlebar Position
- Reach: Slight bend in elbows when in drops/aerobars
- Drop: Comfortable without straining lower back
- Width: Shoulder width or slightly narrower for aero

### Getting a Professional Fit
- Recommended for new bikes
- Re-check after injury or major position changes
- Bring shoes, shorts, and any accessories you ride with
- Cost: $100-300 typically

## Cycling Position and Aerodynamics

### Aerodynamic Basics
At race speeds (15-25 mph), aerodynamic drag is the primary force you're fighting. Position changes have more impact than equipment upgrades.

### Aero Position Elements
- **Flat back:** Reduce torso angle relative to wind
- **Narrow arms:** Bring arms close to body centerline
- **Hidden hands:** Hands near or behind stem (in aerobars)
- **Still head:** Tape helmet to head position
- **Closed gaps:** No gaps between arms and body

### Comfort vs. Aero
The most aero position is useless if you can't hold it. Balance:
- Start conservative with position
- Gradually lower over weeks/months
- Core strength supports aero position
- Rise up for climbs and technical sections

## Pedaling Technique

### Cadence
- **Optimal range:** 80-95 RPM for most athletes
- **Low cadence:** More muscular force, more fatigue
- **High cadence:** More cardiovascular, less muscle fatigue
- **Race pacing:** Higher cadence saves legs for running

### Pedal Stroke Phases
1. **Power phase:** 12 o'clock to 5 o'clock (push down)
2. **Bottom transition:** 5 to 7 o'clock (scrape mud off shoe)
3. **Recovery:** 7 to 11 o'clock (unweight, don't pull up)
4. **Top transition:** 11 to 12 o'clock (drive foot forward)

### Drills for Improvement

#### Single-Leg Drills
- Unclip one foot, pedal with other
- 30 seconds each leg
- Focus on smooth circle
- 3-4 sets during easy rides

#### High Cadence Drills
- Spin at 100-120+ RPM for 1-2 minutes
- Maintain smooth pedal stroke
- Builds neuromuscular coordination

#### Low Cadence Strength
- Heavy gear, 50-60 RPM
- 3-5 minutes at a time
- Builds muscular endurance
- Use on climbs

## Training Types

### Endurance/Base Rides
**Purpose:** Build aerobic base and fat metabolism
- Duration: 1.5-4 hours
- Intensity: 65-75% of max HR or 55-75% FTP
- Terrain: Rolling to flat
- Cadence: Self-selected, typically 80-90 RPM

### Tempo Rides
**Purpose:** Improve sustainable pace
- Duration: 30-90 minutes at tempo
- Intensity: 76-85% max HR or 76-90% FTP
- Terrain: Variable
- Cadence: 85-95 RPM

### Interval Sessions
**Purpose:** Increase VO2max and threshold

#### Threshold Intervals
- Duration: 8-20 minute intervals
- Intensity: 91-105% FTP
- Recovery: 2-5 minutes between
- Sets: 2-4 intervals

#### VO2max Intervals
- Duration: 3-5 minute intervals
- Intensity: 106-120% FTP
- Recovery: Equal to work time
- Sets: 4-6 intervals

#### Short Intervals
- Duration: 30 seconds to 2 minutes
- Intensity: 120-150%+ FTP
- Recovery: 1-3 minutes between
- Sets: 6-12 intervals

### Brick Sessions
**Purpose:** Practice running off the bike
- Bike: Any duration, any intensity
- Run: 10-60 minutes immediately after
- Most important: Transitions between sports
- Frequency: At least once per week

## Power Training (If Using Power Meter)

### Why Power?
- Objective measure of effort
- Pacing for races
- Training intensity zones
- Tracking fitness changes

### Key Metrics

#### Functional Threshold Power (FTP)
- Highest average power sustainable for 60 minutes
- Generally tested as 95% of 20-minute max effort
- Used to calculate training zones

#### Normalized Power (NP)
- Accounts for variability in pace
- Shows physiological cost of variable effort

#### Intensity Factor (IF)
- Ratio of NP to FTP
- Guides race pacing

### Racing with Power

| Distance | Target IF | Power Target |
|----------|-----------|--------------|
| Sprint | 0.95-1.05 | 95-105% FTP |
| Olympic | 0.85-0.95 | 85-95% FTP |
| Quarter | 0.80-0.90 | 80-90% FTP |
| 70.3 | 0.75-0.85 | 75-85% FTP |

## Hill Training

### Climbing Technique
- Stay seated for most climbs (lower heart rate)
- Stand periodically to stretch and change muscles
- Maintain cadence above 60 RPM
- Shift before the climb, not during

### Descent Technique
- Look ahead, not down
- Brake before corners, not during
- Pedal out of corners for speed
- Be aero on descents

### Hill Workout Examples

#### Hill Repeats
- Find 4-8 minute hill
- 3-5 repetitions
- Ride up at threshold
- Easy spin down for recovery

#### Short Power Hills
- Find 1-2 minute steep hill
- 6-10 repetitions
- Seated, high power
- Full recovery between

#### Rolling Course
- Ride continuously on hilly route
- Push on climbs, recover on descents
- Total ride: 1.5-3 hours

## Indoor Training

### Benefits
- Controlled environment
- No weather concerns
- Precise interval execution
- Safety from traffic
- Time efficient

### Trainer Types
- **Wheel-on:** Affordable, more tire wear
- **Direct drive:** Quieter, more accurate, no tire wear
- **Smart trainers:** Variable resistance, interactive software

### Indoor Workout Tips
- Use fan for cooling
- Use raised front wheel (simulates road position)
- Trainer tires for wheel-on trainers
- Limit to 90 minutes maximum typically

### Sample Indoor Workouts

#### Endurance (60 minutes)
```
10 min easy spin
40 min @ endurance pace
10 min easy spin
```

#### Threshold (75 minutes)
```
15 min warm-up
3 x 10 min @ threshold, 5 min recovery
10 min cool-down
```

#### VO2max (60 minutes)
```
15 min warm-up
5 x 4 min @ VO2max, 4 min recovery
15 min cool-down
```

## Group Riding

### Benefits
- Drafting saves 15-30% energy
- Social aspect improves motivation
- Push each other to improve
- Learn from experienced riders

### Drafting Rules
- **Non-drafting triathlon:** Maintain 7m (23ft) distance
- **Draft-legal triathlon:** Drafting allowed
- **Group training:** Drafting acceptable and encouraged

### Safety in Groups
- Call out hazards (potholes, cars)
- Signal turns and stops
- Maintain steady pace (no sudden braking)
- Overlap wheels only when confident

## Nutrition on the Bike

### Why Eat on the Bike?
- Can't consume enough calories on the run
- Bike is easiest place to eat
- Sets up glycogen stores for run

### Timing Strategy
- Begin eating within first 30 minutes
- Aim for 60-90g carbs per hour
- Set timer reminders
- More for longer distances

### Food Options
- Sports drinks (mix of carbs)
- Gels (quick energy, need water)
- Bars/solid food (long rides)
- Chews/gummies (easy to chew)
- Real food for ultra distances (bananas, fig bars)

### Hydration
- Two bottles minimum on bike
- Plan to drink one bottle per hour minimum
- More in heat
- Sodium/electrolytes important for longer/hotter races

## Common Cycling Mistakes

### 1. Training Too Hard on Easy Days
Easy rides should be easy. They build base and aid recovery.

### 2. Skipping Long Rides
Long rides build endurance. Don't substitute with shorter hard rides.

### 3. Poor Bike Fit
Leads to injury and discomfort. Invest in professional fit.

### 4. Neglecting Bike Handling
Cornering, descending, and pack skills matter. Practice regularly.

### 5. Not Practicing Race Day Set-up
Test your race nutrition, clothing, and position before race day.

### 6. Over-racing the Bike
Leaves nothing for the run. Proper pacing comes from experience and discipline.

## Bike Maintenance Basics

### Pre-Ride Check
- Tire pressure (check weekly recommendations)
- Brakes working
- Chain lubrication
- Quick releases tight
- No loose parts

### Regular Maintenance
- Clean bike weekly
- Lubricate chain every 100-200 miles
- Check brake pads monthly
- Tire inspection regularly
- Professional service annually

### Skills to Learn
- Fixing a flat tire (essential!)
- Basic adjustments (derailleurs, brakes)
- Emergency repairs
- Chain cleaning
