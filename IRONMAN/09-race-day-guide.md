# Race Day Guide

## Race Week Preparation

### The Taper

#### Purpose
- Physical: Glycogen storage, tissue repair, hormone restoration
- Mental: Build anticipation, sharpen focus
- Logistics: Prepare all race day elements

#### Typical Taper Timeline

| Distance | Taper Start | Volume Reduction |
|----------|-------------|------------------|
| Sprint | 5-7 days before | 50% |
| Olympic | 7-10 days before | 50-60% |
| Quarter | 7-10 days before | 50-60% |
| 70.3 | 10-14 days before | 40-70% gradually |

#### Taper Principles
- Reduce volume, not intensity (keep some short, hard efforts)
- Maintain frequency of sessions
- Resist urge to do extra (trust the process)
- Expect to feel flat/lethargic (normal during taper)

### Race Week Checklist

#### 7 Days Out
- [ ] Confirm race registration and logistics
- [ ] Review course maps (swim, bike, run)
- [ ] Check weather forecast
- [ ] Finalize nutrition plan
- [ ] Confirm travel arrangements

#### 5-6 Days Out
- [ ] Check all gear (nothing broken, everything fits)
- [ ] Test race bike if traveled
- [ ] Purchase remaining nutrition needs
- [ ] Begin carbo-loading if appropriate

#### 3-4 Days Out
- [ ] Pack for race (use checklist)
- [ ] Review race manual/rules
- [ ] Plan race morning timeline
- [ ] Final gear checks
- [ ] Confirm bike is race-ready

#### 2 Days Out
- [ ] Travel if needed
- [ ] Check in at race site
- [ ] Attend race briefing
- [ ] Review transition area layout
- [ ] Early, easy workout
- [ ] Stay off feet as much as possible

#### 1 Day Out
- [ ] Rack bike (if required day before)
- [ ] Final transition check
- [ ] Drive bike course (or study thoroughly)
- [ ] Prep race morning gear
- [ ] Hydrate normally
- [ ] Light dinner, early bedtime
- [ ] Set multiple alarms

## Race Morning

### Wake-Up Routine

#### Timeline Example (for 7am race start)
- 4:00am: Alarm 1
- 4:15am: Wake up, start hydrating
- 4:30am: Light breakfast
- 5:00am: Leave for venue
- 5:30am: Arrive, set up transition
- 6:00am: Body marking, final prep
- 6:15am: Warm-up
- 6:45am: Enter swim start area
- 7:00am: Race start

#### Race Morning Breakfast
- Eat 2-3 hours before race when possible
- Familiar foods (tested in training)
- 1-3g carbohydrate per kg body weight
- Lower fiber than normal
- Moderate protein, low fat
- Examples: Oatmeal, toast with jam, banana, sports drink

### At the Race Site

#### Setting Up Transition

**Find your spot:**
- Note rack number, row, landmarks
- Walk from swim entry to your rack
- Walk from your rack to bike exit
- Walk from bike entry to your rack
- Walk from your rack to run exit

**Transition Setup:**
- Bike racked by saddle
- Helmet on aerobars with straps open
- Sunglasses in helmet
- Shoes: If pre-clipped, rubber bands to hold in place
- Running shoes with elastic laces, socks ready
- Hat, race belt, nutrition
- Towel to stand on

**Minimal Setup:**
- Less is more
- Only what you need
- Practice this setup before race day

#### Body Marking
- Typically race-provided volunteers
- Numbers on both arms, one leg
- Age on calf
- Some races require timing chip strap

#### Final Warm-Up

**Swim warm-up (if possible):**
- 5-10 minutes easy swimming
- A few short pickups
- Check water temp, get acclimated
- Practice sighting a buoy

**If no swim warm-up:**
- Dynamic stretching
- Arm swings and circles
- Band pull-aparts (if you have bands)
- Light jogging

## The Swim

### At the Start
- Position yourself appropriately:
  - Fast swimmers: Front, inside line
  - Middle: Middle of pack
  - Anxious/slower: Back or outside
- Stay calm during the wait
- Control breathing
- Focus on self, not others

### During the Swim
- Start steady, not sprint
- Find your rhythm early
- Control breathing patterns
- Sight regularly (every 6-12 strokes)
- Stay on course (shorter = faster
- If anxious: Focus on technique, breathe deeper
- Draft if comfortable: Feet or hip of similar-speed swimmer
- If bumped: Keep moving, don't stop

### Swim Exit
- As water gets shallow: Start kicking harder
- Stand when water is waist-deep
- Lift goggles to forehead
- Run to transition (don't rush too much)
- Begin stripping wetsuit if wearing (to waist)

## Transition 1 (Swim to Bike)

### The Transition
1. Run to your rack (know your spot!)
2. Wetsuit down to waist while running if possible
3. At rack: Wetsuit completely off
4. Put on helmet FIRST (required)
5. Sunglasses on
6. Grab bike
7. Run to mount line
8. Mount bike (running mount or regular)

### Common Mistakes
- Forgetting to put helmet on first
- Leaving gear in wrong order
- Not knowing their rack location
- Trying to do too much (change clothes, etc.)

### Speed Tips
- Practice transitions in training
- Have minimal gear
- Know your route to/from rack
- Visualize before race
- Stay calm, don't rush

## The Bike

### Bike Start
- Start in easy gear
- Spin legs up gradually
- Don't sprint out of transition
- Settle into rhythm
- Begin nutrition early (within 20 min)

### Bike Execution

**Pacing:**
- Stick to plan (power, HR, or perceived effort)
- Don't race others (especially early)
- Expect to pass people later who go too hard early
- Use hills and descents appropriately

**Nutrition:**
- Begin eating within first 30 minutes
- Follow your practiced plan
- Hydrate consistently
- Set reminders if needed
- Prepare for the run

**Course Management:**
- Know the key sections (hills, technical)
- Stay aero as much as possible
- Corner safely
- Pass legally (non-drafting: complete pass within 25m)
- Be aware of others on course

### Bike End
- Up gears for transition
- Feet out of shoes in last 500m (advanced) OR
- Wait until transition to remove shoes
- Dismount before dismount line

## Transition 2 (Bike to Run)

### The Transition
1. Rack bike in correct spot
2. Helmet OFF (after bike racked)
3. Remove bike shoes if not done on course
4. Put on running shoes
5. Grab hat, race belt, nutrition
6. Exit to run course

### Running Out
- Legs will feel awkward (normal!)
- Start with shorter strides
- Focus on quick cadence
- Don't worry about pace for first 0.5 mile
- Settle into rhythm

## The Run

### Run Strategy

**Start (0-1 mile):**
- Controlled start, not a sprint
- Let body adjust
- Focus on rhythm and cadence
- Ignore pace initially (it will settle)

**Middle Miles:**
- Find your rhythm and pace
- Execute nutrition plan
- Break into segments mentally
- Use aid stations efficiently

**Final Miles:**
- Continue nutrition
- Draw energy from spectators
- Pick off people in front of you
- Increase effort as you approach finish
- Strong finish

### Aid Stations
- Know what's offered (water, sports drink, gels)
- Know when they occur on course
- Take what you need
- Don't linger
- Thank volunteers

### Running Form Focus
- Quick feet (170+ spm)
- Upright posture
- Relaxed shoulders
- Landing under center of mass
- Eyes forward

### Dealing with the Run
- Walk through aid stations if needed
- Run between landmarks
- Positive self-talk
- Everyone is hurting
- You can do hard things

## The Finish

### Last Mile
- Give what you have left
- Feed off crowd energy
- Strong form
- Mental focus on finish line

### Crossing the Line
- Celebrate (you earned it!)
- Accept medal
- Grab water/nutrition
- Don't collapse (keep moving gently)
- Find friends/family

### Post-Finish
- Begin rehydration
- Eat within 30-60 minutes
- Cool down gradually
- Don't sit immediately
- Check results later (enjoy the moment first)
- Recovery nutrition and rest

## Race Day Problem Solving

### Mechanical Issues

**Flat tire:**
- Pull over safely
- Change tube (practice before race!)
- CO2 or pump
- Check for debris in tire
- Continue

**Dropped chain:**
- Shift to small ring
- Pedal gently
- Hand shift if needed

**Other mechanicals:**
- Race support may help
- Sometimes DNF is the only option (accept it)

### Nutrition Problems

**Can't eat:**
- Switch products
- Slow down
- Focus on hydration
- Wait for stomach to settle

**Stomach issues:**
- Slow down
- Reduce intake temporarily
- Water can help
- Wait for symptoms to pass

### Pacing Problems

**Went out too hard:**
- Acknowledge it
- Reduce effort now
- Salvage what you can
- Learn for next time

**Feeling better than expected:**
- Don't massively increase effort
- Slight increase okay
- Save something for emergencies
- Trust the plan

### Mental Challenges

**Panic in swim:**
- Breathe (maybe breaststroke briefly)
- Focus on technique
- Move to edge/side
- Collect yourself, then continue

**Low point:**
- One step at a time
- Next aid station, next mile
- This is temporary
- You've trained for this

## Post-Race

### Immediate (0-2 hours)
- Walk/move gently
- Begin rehydration
- Recovery nutrition within 60 min
- Change into comfortable clothes
- Celebrate with supporters

### Short-Term (2-24 hours)
- Continue hydration and eating
- Rest and/or active recovery
- Compression garments if desired
- Light movement the next day
- Process emotions

### Reflection (2-7 days)
- Review race (what worked, what didn't)
- Look at data if you tracked
- Write lessons learned
- Save memories (photos, medals, etc.)
- Plan next steps after rest
