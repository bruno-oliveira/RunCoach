# Complete Nutrition and Hydration Guide for Triathlon

## Why Nutrition is the "Fourth Discipline"

Proper nutrition and hydration can make or break your race. You can train perfectly for months, but poor fueling on race day will undermine everything. Nutrition affects energy levels, recovery, mental clarity, and overall performance.

## Daily Nutrition Philosophy

### The Athlete's Plate

Your daily nutrition should match your training load:

#### Rest Day
- 50% vegetables and fruits
- 25% lean protein
- 25% whole grains/starchy vegetables

#### Moderate Training Day
- 50% vegetables and fruits
- 25% lean protein
- 25% whole grains/starchy vegetables
- Plus training fuel

#### Hard/Long Training Day
- 50% vegetables and fruits
- 25% lean protein
- 25% whole grains/starchy vegetables
- Plus extra carbohydrates and training fuel

### Macronutrient Overview

#### Carbohydrates
- **Purpose:** Primary fuel source for endurance activity
- **Sources:** Grains, potatoes, rice, fruits, vegetables
- **Timing:** Before, during, and after training sessions
- **Daily needs:** 5-12g per kg body weight (varies by training load)

#### Protein
- **Purpose:** Muscle repair and recovery, immune function
- **Sources:** Lean meats, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, soy
- **Timing:** Post-workout, distributed through the day
- **Daily needs:** 1.2-2.0g per kg body weight

#### Fat
- **Purpose:** Hormone production, fat-soluble vitamin absorption
- **Sources:** Nuts, seeds, avocados, olive oil, fatty fish
- **Timing:** Mostly in meals away from training
- **Daily needs:** 20-35% of total calories

### Micronutrient Focus

#### Key Vitamins/Minerals
- **Iron:** Oxygen transport (especially for women)
- **Vitamin D:** Bone health, immunity
- **Calcium:** Bone health
- **Magnesium:** Muscle function, sleep
- **B vitamins:** Energy metabolism
- **Zinc:** Immunity, recovery

#### Food Quality Priorities
1. Colorful vegetables and fruits (antioxidants)
2. Whole grains over refined
3. Lean proteins
4. Healthy fats
5. Minimized processed foods

## Pre-Workout Nutrition

### Timing and Content

#### 2-4 Hours Before
- Full meal
- 1-2g carbs per kg body weight
- Moderate protein
- Low fat and fiber
- Examples: Oatmeal with banana, toast with peanut butter and fruit

#### 30-60 Minutes Before
- Light snack
- 0.5g carbs per kg body weight
- Easy to digest
- Examples: Banana, energy bar, toast

#### 15-30 Minutes Before (if needed)
- Very light
- Fast-digesting carbs
- Examples: Energy gel, sports drink, piece of fruit

### Pre-Workout Meal Examples

| Meal | Carbs | Protein | Fat | Timing |
|------|-------|---------|-----|--------|
| Oatmeal with banana + honey | High | Low | Low | 2-3 hrs |
| Toast with PB + fruit | Mod | Mod | Mod | 2-3 hrs |
| Fruit + yogurt | Mod | Mod | Low | 1 hrs |
| Banana | Mod | None | None | 30 min |
| Sports drink | High | None | None | 15 min |

## During Workout Nutrition

### What and When to Fuel

#### Sessions Under 60 Minutes
- Water only (or electrolyte drink in heat)
- No calories typically needed

#### Sessions 60-90 Minutes
- Water or electrolyte drink
- Optional: Light carb intake

#### Sessions 90+ Minutes
- Electrolyte drink essential
- Carbohydrates needed: 30-60g per hour
- Practice fueling in training

#### Sessions 2.5+ Hours
- Carbohydrates: 60-90g per hour
- Mix of glucose and fructose for better absorption
- Start fueling early, don't wait to feel hungry

### What to Consume During Training

#### Sports Drinks
- Provides electrolytes and carbs
- Easy to consume
- 30-60g carbs per typical bottle
- Best for: Steady intake, heat

#### Energy Gels
- Concentrated carbs
- Quick energy
- Must consume with water
- 20-30g carbs per gel
- Best for: Long rides, runs

#### Energy Bars/Chews
- Solid food option
- Mix of macronutrients
- 25-45g carbs per serving
- Best for: Long bike rides

#### Real Food Options
- Bananas, dates, fig bars
- Rice cakes with honey/jam
- Mashed potatoes (in race nutrition)
- Best for: Low-intensity long sessions

### Carb Sources and Mixing

#### Single-Source Carbs
- Glucose-only products: Max ~60g/hour
- More can cause GI distress

#### Multiple Transportable Carbs
- Mix of glucose + fructose
- Can absorb 90g+/hour
- Ratio: 2:1 glucose to fructose typically
- Better for long/hard efforts

## Hydration Strategy

### Daily Hydration
- Target: Half body weight (lbs) in ounces daily
- More with training
- Urine color: Pale yellow ideal
- Don't rely on thirst alone

### During Training
- 16-24 oz per hour general guideline
- Varies with heat, humidity, intensity
- Drink to thirst, but don't fall behind
- Practice drinking while moving

### Electrolytes
- Sodium, potassium, magnesium, calcium
- Lost through sweat
- Critical for: Fluid balance, cramp prevention

#### Sodium Needs
- Average: 500-700mg/hour
- Heavy sweaters: 1000mg+/hour
- Test in training
- Salt tabs or electrolyte drinks

### Hydration Assessment

| Urine Color | Hydration Status |
|-------------|------------------|
| Clear/pale yellow | Well hydrated |
| Yellow | Adequately hydrated |
| Dark yellow | Needs more fluids |
| Amber/brown | Dehydrated - drink now |

## Post-Workout Nutrition

### The Recovery Window

#### Immediate (0-30 minutes)
- Carbs: 1-1.2g per kg body weight
- Protein: 20-30g
- Begin rehydration

#### Short Term (30-60 minutes)
- Complete recovery meal/snack
- Continue hydration
- This window optimizes recovery

#### Optimal Recovery Foods
- Chocolate milk (classic recovery drink)
- Protein shake with banana
- Greek yogurt with fruit
- Turkey/hummus sandwich
- Rice bowl with protein

### Post-Workout Meal Examples

| Food | Carbs (g) | Protein (g) | Calories |
|------|-----------|-------------|----------|
| Chocolate milk 16oz | 52 | 16 | 340 |
| Greek yogurt 1 cup + banana | 35 | 20 | 220 |
| Protein shake + fruit | 40 | 25 | 300 |
| Turkey sandwich | 35 | 25 | 350 |
| Rice bowl with chicken | 60 | 30 | 500 |

## Race Week Nutrition

### The Week Before

#### Carbohydrate Loading (for 70.3 and longer)
- Days 7-4: Moderate carb intake
- Days 3-1: High carb intake (7-10g/kg body weight)
- Don't overeat - just shift proportions
- Reduce fiber in final 48 hours

#### Daily Targets
- Carbs: Higher than normal
- Protein: Normal to slightly high
- Fat: Moderate to low
- Fiber: Reduce in final 48 hours

### Day Before Race

#### Meals
- Breakfast: Normal carb-rich meal
- Lunch: Largest meal of the day (early afternoon)
- Dinner: Light, easy to digest, 12+ hours before race
- Avoid: High fiber, high fat, spicy, new foods

#### Hydration
- Drink normally throughout day
- Don't over-drink (clear urine is enough)
- Electrolyte drink with dinner

### Race Morning

#### Timeline Before Race Start

| Time Before | Action |
|-------------|--------|
| 3-4 hours | Light breakfast if possible |
| 2-3 hours | Normal breakfast (2-3g carbs/kg) |
| 1 hour | Top-up snack or sports drink |
| 15-30 min | Water or gel if needed |
| 10 min | Nothing (avoid swim issues) |

#### Breakfast Examples
- Oatmeal with banana and honey
- Toast with jam + fruit
- Energy bar + sports drink
- Bagel with cream cheese
- Rice with egg

## Race Day Fueling

### Swim
- None during swim
- Pre-swim meal provides fuel
- Settle stomach in transition

### Bike (Primary Fueling Opportunity)

#### Sprint
- Water only usually
- Maybe small electrolyte drink

#### Olympic
- 20-40g carbs on the bike
- 1-2 bottles of sports drink or 1-2 gels

#### Quarter Ironman
- 45-60g carbs on bike
- Sports drink + gel or bar

#### 70.3
- 60-90g carbs per hour
- Start eating within 30 minutes
- Mix drinks, gels, and solids
- Target: 400-600 calories/hour

### Run

#### Sprint
- Water at aid stations
- Minimal if any calories

#### Olympic
- Water + electrolyte drink
- Maybe one gel mid-run

#### 70.3
- Continue fueling: 30-60g carbs/hour
- Gels easier to consume than solids
- Aid station offerings
- Coke in final miles (if practiced)

### Nutrition Targets by Distance

| Distance | Bike Calories/Hour | Run Calories/Hour | Total Carb/Hour |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Sprint | 0-30g | 0-15g | Minimal |
| Olympic | 30-50g | 15-30g | 45-80g |
| Quarter | 40-60g | 20-40g | 60-100g |
| 70.3 | 60-90g | 30-60g | 90-150g |

## GI Distress Prevention

### Common Issues
- Nausea
- Cramping
- Diarrhea
- Bloating
- Reflux

### Prevention Strategies

#### Training-Related
- Practice race nutrition many times
- Train at race intensity with nutrition
- Nothing new on race day
- Train in heat if race will be hot

#### Nutrition-Related
- Avoid high-fiber foods pre-race
- Don't over-fuel (stick to practiced amounts)
- Stay hydrated (dehydration worsens GI issues)
- Start fueling early, don't catch up

#### Product-Related
- Test all products in training
- Mix carb sources (glucose + fructose)
- Avoid high fat/protein during exercise
- Consider caffeine sensitivity

### If GI Issues Strike During Race
- Slow down (lower intensity easier on gut)
- Reduce fueling temporarily
- Increase hydration
- Wait for symptoms to pass before resuming normal fueling

## Caffeine and Performance

### Benefits
- Reduced perception of effort
- Enhanced fat oxidation
- Improved focus and alertness
- Performance improvement: 1-3%

### Timing and Dosing
- Pre-exercise: 3-6mg per kg body weight, 60 min before
- During exercise: 1-2mg per kg body weight per hour
- Sources: Coffee, gels with caffeine, cola

### Considerations
- Practice in training
- Some athletes are non-responders
- Avoid daily high intake (develops tolerance)
- May affect sleep if late in day

## Weight Management Considerations

### During Training
- Weight loss and performance training are difficult to combine
- Focus on performance nutrition during key training blocks
- Periodize weight management (off-season, base phase)
- Never sacrifice recovery nutrition for weight goals

### Healthy Approach
- Small calorie deficit (300-500 calories) if weight loss desired
- Maintain adequate protein for muscle maintenance
- Don't skip recovery nutrition after key sessions
- Focus on food quality over calorie counting

## Alcohol and Performance

### Effects on Performance
- Dehydration
- Poor sleep quality
- Reduced recovery
- Impaired reaction time
- Empty calories

### Recommendations
- Limit or avoid during peak training
- Never before long training days
- If consuming: Stay hydrated, moderate amounts
- Know your personal tolerance

## Creating Your Nutrition Plan

### Step 1: Assess Needs
- Training volume and intensity
- Race distance and expected duration
- Personal sweat rate
- GI tolerance history

### Step 2: Choose Products and Foods
- Sports drinks you tolerate
- Gels and other carb sources
- Real food options for long sessions
- Pre-race breakfast that works

### Step 3: Practice
- Every long training session
- Every race simulation brick
- Various conditions (heat, intensity)
- Record what works and doesn't

### Step 4: Finalize Race Plan
- Exact products and amounts
- Timing of intake
- Backup options if GI issues
- Aid station plan for race

### Step 5: Execute
- Trust your training
- Stick to the plan on race day
- Be flexible if something goes wrong
