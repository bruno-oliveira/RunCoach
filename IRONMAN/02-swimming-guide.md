# Complete Swimming Guide for Triathlon

## Why Swimming Matters

Swimming typically represents 10-15% of total race time but can set the tone for your entire race. A comfortable, efficient swim leaves you energized for the bike and run. A panicked, exhausting swim can ruin your race before it truly begins.

## The Challenge for Beginners

Most triathlon newcomers find swimming the hardest discipline because:
- Technical skill matters more than fitness
- Water is an unfamiliar environment
- Open water adds anxiety factors
- Few adults have recent swimming experience

## Pool Basics

### Essential Equipment
- **Goggles:** Must-fit, anti-fog, outdoor vs. indoor lens options
- **Swim cap:** Reduces drag, keeps hair contained, required in races
- **Fins:** Training aid for technique drills (not racing)
- **Pull buoy:** Float between legs to isolate upper body
- **Kickboard:** For kick-focused drills

### Pool vs. Open Water Swimming

| Pool | Open Water |
|------|------------|
| Clear water visibility | Limited visibility |
| Lane lines for guidance | No lines, sighting required |
| Walls for rest/push-offs | No walls, continuous swimming |
| Controlled temperature | Variable temperature |
| Predictable environment | Currents, waves, chop |

## Swimming Technique Fundamentals

### Body Position
**Goal:** High, flat body position at the water's surface

Key elements:
- Head position: Look down, not forward (water line at top of head)
- Hips: Should float near surface
- Legs: Minimal kick, feet near surface
- Core: Engaged to maintain alignment

### The Catch and Pull
1. **Entry:** Hand enters water in front of shoulder, fingers first
2. **Extension:** Arm reaches forward, slight body rotation
3. **Catch:** Fingertips point down, elbow high
4. **Pull:** Draw water back, keeping elbow high
5. **Push:** Accelerate hand past hip
6. **Recovery:** Elbow leads, relaxed hand

### Breathing
- **Timing:** Breathe during the rotation, when one arm is extended
- **Duration:** Quick inhale, face returns to water
- **Pattern:** Every 2, 3, or 4 strokes (bilateral preferred)
- **Head position:** One goggle in water, not lifting head

### The Kick
- **Purpose:** Balance and body position, not propulsion
- **Technique:** Kick from hips, not knees; ankles flexible
- **Frequency:** 2-beat (efficient) to 6-beat (sprint)
- **Common error:** Over-kicking wastes energy

## Stroke Timing and Rhythm

### Rotation/Body Roll
- 45-degree rotation to each side
- Rotation powers the stroke
- Minimal time flat on stomach
- Creates longer stroke reach

### Stroke Rate
- Distance: 18-24 strokes per length (25yd pool)
- Sprint: Higher rate acceptable
- Efficiency over power for triathlon

## Key Drills for Improvement

### 1. Catch-Up Drill
**Purpose:** Extension and timing
**Execution:** Wait for one hand to "catch up" to the other before next stroke
**Focus:** Full extension, stable body

### 2. Single Arm Drill
**Purpose:** Pull technique and rotation
**Execution:** Swim using one arm, other arm extended or at side
**Focus:** High elbow catch, full pull, body rotation

### 3. Fist Drill
**Purpose:** Elbow position and forearm catch
**Execution:** Swim with closed fists
**Focus:** Feeling the water with forearm, keeping elbow high

### 4. Fingertip Drag
**Purpose:** Recovery mechanics
**Execution:** Drag fingertips across water surface during recovery
**Focus:** High elbow, relaxed hand, controlled movement

### 5. Side Kick
**Purpose:** Body position and rotation
**Execution:** Kick on side, one arm extended, one at side
**Focus:** Horizontal body line, head position

### 6. 6-1-6 Drill
**Purpose:** Rotation timing
**Execution:** 6 kicks on side, 1 stroke, 6 kicks on other side
**Focus:** Controlled rotation, stable position

## Structuring Swim Sessions

### Session Components
1. **Warm-up:** 200-400m easy swimming with drills
2. **Main set:** Intervals with specific purpose
3. **Cool-down:** 100-200m easy swimming

### Workout Types

#### Technique Session
Focus on form over fitness
- High proportion of drill work
- Short distances (25-50m)
- Rest fully between efforts
- Example: 10 x 25m catch-up drill

#### Endurance Session
Build swimming fitness
- Longer intervals (100-400m)
- Short rest (10-20 seconds per 100m)
- Steady pace throughout
- Example: 4 x 200m @ moderate pace

#### Threshold/Speed Session
Improve sustained speed
- Medium intervals (50-200m)
- Moderate rest (20-30 seconds)
- Challenging but sustainable pace
- Example: 10 x 100m @ threshold pace

### Sample Workouts by Level

#### Beginner (1500-2000m total)
```
Warm-up: 200m easy
  4 x 25m drill (catch-up, side kick)
Main: 8 x 50m @ moderate pace, RI 0:15
  4 x 100m @ easy pace, RI 0:20
Cool-down: 100m easy
```

#### Intermediate (2000-3000m total)
```
Warm-up: 300m easy, 4 x 25m drill
Main: 3 x 100m @ moderate, RI 0:10
  6 x 75m @ threshold, RI 0:20
  4 x 50m @ fast, RI 0:30
  200m easy recovery
Cool-down: 200m choice
```

#### Advanced (3000-5000m total)
```
Warm-up: 400m, 6 x 25m drill
Main: 4 x 200m @ threshold, RI 0:20
  8 x 100m @ faster threshold, RI 0:15
  12 x 50m @ VO2max, RI 0:20
  200m easy
Cool-down: 200m easy
```

## Open Water Swimming

### Skills Required for Open Water
- Sighting: Lifting head to see direction
- Navigation: Following course markers
- Pack swimming: Swimming near others
- Entries/exits: Running start, beach exit
- Wetsuit swimming: Different body position

### Sighting Technique
1. Lift eyes only (not whole head) during stroke
2. Look forward during recovery phase
3. Spot landmarks or buoys
4. Sight every 6-12 strokes
5. Practice in pool first

### Wetsuit Swimming
- Buoyancy changes body position
- Arms may fatigue faster due to rubber resistance
- Practice swimming in wetsuit before race day
- Use body glide on neck/wrists to prevent chafing
- Practice removal for T1

### Open Water Practice Sessions

**Pool Simulation:**
- Swim without touching walls (turn at flags)
- Practice sighting every 6 strokes
- Start in middle of lane, no wall push
- Swim with eyes closed briefly to test straightness

**Open Water Sessions:**
- Start with supervised group sessions
- Use brightly colored swim cap
- Stay close to shore initially
- Have kayak/paddleboard support if possible

### Safety Considerations
- Never swim alone in open water
- Use a swim buoy for visibility
- Know water temperature
- Check for currents before entering
- Have exit strategy
- Know hypothermia signs

## Overcoming Swim Anxiety

### Common Fears
- Deep water
- Limited visibility
- Swimming in crowds
- Not touching bottom
- Open water creatures

### Strategies
1. **Gradual exposure:** Start in shallow, progress deeper
2. **Skills first:** Build confidence in pool
3. **Group swimming:** Join organized open water sessions
4. **Mental techniques:** Focus on technique, not fear
5. **Preparation:** Know the course, conditions

## Race Day Swimming

### Pre-Race Swim Warm-up
Even with time_trial start formats, a brief warm-up helps:
- 5-10 minutes easy swimming
- A few short pickups (15-20 strokes fast)
- Practice sighting
- Check water temperature

### Positioning
- Faster swimmers: Front, toward inside line
- Middle swimmers: Middle of pack
- Slower swimmers: Back or to the outside
- Anxious swimmers: Back or edge for space

### Race Execution
- Start conservatively (avoid sprint)
- Find your rhythm early
- Draft behind similar-speed swimmers
- Sight regularly
- Stay calm if bumped
- Save energy for rest of race

### Exit to T1
- As you approach exit, kick harder to get blood to legs
- Stand when water is waist-deep
- Don't run too early (wastes energy)
- Lift goggles to forehead while running to transition

## Common Swimming Mistakes

### 1. Over-kicking
Burns energy that should be saved for bike/run. Kick should maintain body position, not provide propulsion.

### 2. Lifting Head to Breathe
Lifts hips, creates drag. Rotate to breathe, keep one goggle in water.

### 3. Crossing Over
Hand crossing centerline causes misalignment. Hands should stay wide of centerline.

### 4. Dropping Elbow
Reduces power. High elbow catch uses larger muscle groups.

### 5. Swimming Too Fast in Training
Most swim training should be done at moderate pace. Build technique first.

## Progress Checks

Track improvement with periodic tests:

### 500m Time Trial
Measure sustained swim speed improvement season to season.

### Critical Swim Speed (CSS)
Calculate from two time trials:
- 200m at maximum
- 400m at maximum
Use formula to determine training paces.

### Stroke Count Test
Count strokes per 25m. Lower count = better efficiency.
