# Complete Gear and Equipment Guide

## Gear Philosophy

You don't need expensive equipment to complete a triathlon. The most important gear is the gear that fits you properly and allows you to train consistently and race confidently. Start with essentials, upgrade as you commit to the sport.

## Distance-Based Recommendations

| Distance | Essential Gear | Nice to Have | Advanced |
|----------|----------------|--------------|----------|
| Sprint | Basic kit | Wetsuit | Tri bike |
| Olympic | Wetsuit | Basic aero | Power meter |
| 70.3 | Wetsuit, bike upgrades | Tri bike, aero gear | Full aero setup |

## Swimming Gear

### Essential

#### Goggles
- **Purpose:** Visibility, eye protection
- **Features to look for:**
  - Good seal (no leaks)
  - Anti-fog coating
  - UV protection for open water
  - Tinted for outdoor, clear for indoor/poor light
- **Fit test:** Press to face without strap, should stay in place
- **Budget:** $20-50

#### Swim Cap
- **Purpose:** Reduce drag, keep hair contained
- **Types:**
  - Silicone (more durable, comfortable)
  - Latex (cheaper, thinner)
- **Note:** Most races provide a cap

#### Swimsuit/Trisuit
- **Purpose:** Swim in race-day clothing
- **Options:**
  - Trisuit (single piece for all three sports)
  - Two-piece (tri top + tri shorts)
- **Features:** Quick-dry, minimal padding, snug fit
- **Budget:** $50-150

### Recommended

#### Wetsuit
- **Purpose:** Warmth, buoyancy, speed
- **When needed:** Water temp under ~78°F (varies by race rules)
- **Types:**
  - Full-sleeve (warmest, most buoyant)
  - Sleeveless (more range of motion)
- **Key features:**
  - Thickness: 3-5mm (thicker = more buoyant)
  - Fit: Snug but not restrictive
  - Buoyancy panels for body position
- **Budget:** $150-600

#### Training Aids
- **Pull buoy:** Isolate upper body, improve body position
- **Fins:** Build ankle flexibility, speed
- **Kickboard:** Kick-focused drills
- **Paddles:** Build strength (use carefully)
- **Snorkel:** Focus on technique without breathing rotation
- **Budget:** $20-50 each

## Cycling Gear

### Essential

#### Bike
- **Purpose:** The bike leg (obviously!)
- **Types:**
  - Road bike (versatile, good for beginners)
  - Triathlon/TT bike (aerodynamic, specialized)
- **Minimum requirements:**
  - Proper fit for your body
  - Working gears and brakes
  - Tires in good condition
- **Budget:** $500-3000+ (used market great for beginners)

#### Helmet
- **Purpose:** Safety (required in all races)
- **Types:**
  - Road helmet (versatile, ventilated)
  - Aero helmet (faster, less ventilated)
- **Must:** CPSC certified (or regional equivalent)
- **Budget:** $50-300

#### Bike Shorts/Cycling Shorts
- **Purpose:** Comfort on the saddle
- **Features:**
  - Padded chamois
  - Tight fit
  - Quick-dry material
- **Budget:** $40-150
- **Note:** Tri shorts have less padding (better for running)

#### Water Bottles
- **Purpose:** Hydration on the bike
- **Types:**
  - Standard bottle cages + bottles
  - Aero hydration systems (between aerobars)
  - Behind-saddle systems
- **Budget:** $10-50+

### Recommended

#### Bike Shoes
- **Purpose:** Better power transfer
- **Types:**
  - Road shoes (walkable)
  - Tri-specific shoes (easy on/off, heel loops)
- **Compatibility:** Match cleats to pedals
- **Budget:** $75-300

#### Pedal System
- **Purpose:** Secure foot attachment
- **Types:**
  - Platform pedals (beginner)
  - Clipless with cleats (standard for racing)
- **Budget:** $50-200

#### Bike Computer/GPS
- **Purpose:** Pace, distance, navigation
- **Features:**
  - Speed/distance tracking
  - Heart rate compatible
  - Power meter compatible
  - Navigation on some models
- **Budget:** $100-500

#### Indoor Trainer
- **Purpose:** Training in bad weather, structured workouts
- **Types:**
  - Wheel-on (budget)
  - Direct drive (quieter, more accurate)
  - Smart trainer (resistance control, interactive)
- **Budget:** $150-1200+

### Advanced

#### Aerobars
- **Purpose:** Aerodynamic position
- **Options:**
  - Clip-on (add to road bike)
  - Integrated (tri bike)
- **Budget:** $75-300

#### Power Meter
- **Purpose:** Objective measurement of effort
- **Types:**
  - Pedal-based
  - Crank-based
  - Hub-based
- **Budget:** $400-1500

#### Race Wheels
- **Purpose:** Aerodynamics
- **Features:** Deep rim (50-90mm), carbon construction
- **Budget:** $800-3000 per wheel

#### Aero Helmet
- **Purpose:** Reduced drag
- **Features:** Smooth shape, tail design
- **Budget:** $150-400

## Running Gear

### Essential

#### Running Shoes
- **Purpose:** Protect feet, provide cushioning
- **Selection:**
  - Visit running store for gait analysis
  - Fit for comfort and injury prevention, not looks
  - Consider neutral, stability, or motion control
  - Half-size larger than street shoes often better
- **Lifespan:** 300-500 miles
- **Budget:** $80-180

#### Running Clothing
- **Purpose:** Comfort during training and racing
- **Features:**
  - Moisture-wicking fabric
  - Minimal seams (prevent chafing)
  - Quick-dry
- **Budget:** $30-100 per item

#### Race Belt
- **Purpose:** Hold race number
- **Features:** Easy on/off, elastic
- **Budget:** $5-15

### Recommended

#### Running Watch
- **Purpose:** Pace, distance, heart rate
- **Features:**
  - GPS tracking
  - Heart rate monitor
  - Multi-sport modes (for tri-specific watches)
- **Budget:** $150-600

#### Running Hat/Visor
- **Purpose:** Sun protection, sweat management
- **Options:**
  - Hat (more coverage)
  - Visor (better cooling)
- **Budget:** $15-40

#### Sunglasses
- **Purpose:** Eye protection, visibility
- **Features:**
  - UV protection
  - Good coverage
  - Don't bounce when running
- **Budget:** $30-200

#### Socks
- **Purpose:** Prevent blisters
- **Features:**
  - Moisture-wicking
  - Proper fit
  - Minimal seams
- **Budget:** $10-25/pair

## Transition Gear

### Essential

#### Transition Bag
- **Purpose:** Organize and transport gear
- **Features:**
  - Multiple compartments
  - Wet/dry separation
  - Easy access
- **Budget:** $30-100

#### Tri Suit
- **Purpose:** Single outfit for entire race
- **Features:**
  - Quick-dry fabric
  - Minimal padding (thin chamois)
  - Secure fit
- **Types:**
  - One-piece
  - Two-piece
- **Budget:** $75-250

#### Body Glide/Anti-Chafe
- **Purpose:** Prevent chafing
- **Where to apply:**
  - Neck (wetsuit rub)
  - Underarms
  - Thighs/groin
  - Anywhere friction occurs
- **Budget:** $8-15

#### Towel
- **Purpose:** Dry feet in T1, mark transition spot
- **Tip:** Bright colored towel helps find your spot
- **Budget:** $10-20

### Recommended

#### Wetsuit Lubricant
- **Purpose:** Prevent wetsuit chafing, easier removal
- **Where:** Neck, wrists, ankles
- **Budget:** $10-20

#### Elastic Laces
- **Purpose:** Quick entry in running shoes
- **Brands:** Lock Laces, Yankz, etc.
- **Budget:** $5-10

## Miscellaneous Gear

### Training

#### Swim bag
- Dedicated bag for swim gear
- Wet compartment for suit/towel

#### Bike pump
- Floor pump for home
- Frame pump/CO2 for rides

#### Bike tools
- Multi-tool
- Tire levers
- Spare tubes

#### Heart rate monitor
- Chest strap (most accurate)
- Optical wrist (more convenient)

### Race Day

#### Timing chip strap
- Some races provide, some require your own
- Velcro strap for ankle

#### Special needs bags (longer races)
- Race-provided at most events
- Mark clearly

#### Sunscreen
- Apply before race
- Consider stick format for transitions

#### Duct tape
- Emergency repairs
- Marking transition area

 bike shoes: $150

## Gear Maintenance

### Bike
- Clean weekly
- Lubricate chain every 100-200 miles
- Check tires regularly
- Annual professional service

### Wetsuit
- Rinse with fresh water after use
- Dry inside-out, away from direct sun
- Store flat or on wide hanger

### Running Shoes
- Alternate pairs to let cushioning recover
- Replace at 300-500 miles
- Track mileage

## Final Gear Tips

### 1. Don't Buy Everything at Once
Build your kit over time as you learn what you need.

### 2. Fit Over Price
Well-fitting budget gear beats ill-fitting premium gear.

### 3. Test Before Racing
Never race in untested gear.

### 4. Rent Before Buying (for big items)
Many shops rent wetsuits, aero wheels.

### 5. Used Market is Your Friend
Bikes, wetsuits, and trainers can be found at significant discounts.
